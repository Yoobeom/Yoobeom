# Global vs. Local Process Variation: Decomposition, Physics, Statistical Compact Models, and LVF Representation (device/process/compact-model side)

**Scope of these notes.** Device/process/compact-model view of how variation is split into global (lot-to-lot, wafer-to-wafer, die-to-die = "inter-die") and local (within-die: systematic/spatial + random/mismatch = "intra-die") components, how the two are related physically and statistically, how foundry statistical SPICE models and LVF libraries carry them, and how both can be reflected simultaneously in a timing margin. SSTA/EDA-tool internals are covered by a separate researcher.

## Key Question 1 — How is process variation decomposed into global (L2L/W2W/D2D) and local (within-die systematic + random) components, and how are the components estimated from test structures?

### Takeaway
The industry-standard decomposition is an additive, nested (hierarchical) model in which each level (lot, wafer, die, within-die systematic, within-die random) contributes an independent variance term so that σ²_total = σ²_L2L + σ²_W2W + σ²_D2D + σ²_WID,sys + σ²_WID,rand; in practice all "inter-die" terms are lumped into one global term and the within-die terms into one local term, giving the ubiquitous σ²_T = σ²_G + σ²_L that foundry corner/Monte-Carlo models and LVF libraries are built on. The formal spatial-decomposition method (Stine/Boning/Chung 1997) separates wafer-level, die-level (repeating die pattern), wafer–die interaction and residual random terms, and nested-ANOVA variance-component estimation from addressable test structures (transistor arrays, ring oscillators) is the standard way to measure the terms.

### Inferences
- The nested model is what justifies both (a) foundry "global corner + local Monte Carlo" model structure and (b) LVF-in-PVT-corner libraries: if the levels are independent, the variance terms add, the global terms can be collapsed to one shared random vector (or its N-σ corner), and the local terms to per-instance independent draws. Formally, for parameter p of device i on die d: p_{d,i} = p_0 + g_d + s(x_i,y_i) + r_{d,i}, with Var = σ²_G + σ²_S + σ²_R; the systematic within-die term s(x,y) is either modelled as a spatially-correlated random field (Friedberg/Xiong) or, in foundry practice, folded into the local term as "distance/spatial effects" (TSMC SISPAD 2012) `[inference from V items]`.
- Bowman's result (WID shifts the mean of FMAX, D2D sets its variance) is the statistical reason why signoff separates the two: the global term is handled by picking a corner (it moves everything together, so a deterministic shift is exact for that die), while the local term must be handled statistically along a path (max-of-N and RSS effects) — corners cannot represent it without pessimism `[inference]`.

## Key Question 2 — What is the physics of local random variation (mismatch), how is it modelled (Pelgrom, RDF, LER, MGG/WFV, FinFET/GAA sources), how does the local sigma depend on the global operating point/corner, and are global and local components independent?

### Takeaway
Local random variation is modelled by the Pelgrom area law σ²(ΔP) = A_P²/(WL) + S_P²·D² (random term ∝ 1/√area plus a distance/gradient term), whose physical drivers are random dopant fluctuation (planar), line-edge/fin-edge roughness, metal-gate work-function granularity (HKMG, FinFET, GAA) and fin/sheet geometry fluctuation; the mismatch *parameter* sigma (e.g., σ_Vt) is usually taken as independent of the global corner, but the *delay* sigma it produces is strongly corner- and VDD-dependent (delay sensitivity to ΔVt grows as overdrive shrinks, becoming non-linear/non-Gaussian near 0.5 V), so local timing sigma must be characterized at each global corner. Global and local components are treated as statistically independent in essentially all foundry models, but there is documented evidence (patents, practitioner notes) that the local sigma can be correlated with global shifts and that global N/P Vt are only weakly correlated.

### Inferences
- Sensitivity view of "local sigma at a global corner": with delay D(V_t^G + ΔV_t^L, V_DD), σ_D,local ≈ |∂D/∂V_t|_{corner}·σ_Vt,local. For a velocity-saturated/alpha-power device D ∝ 1/(V_DD − V_t)^α, ∂D/∂V_t /D = α/(V_DD − V_t), so the *relative* local delay sigma grows as the overdrive shrinks — largest at the SS global corner (higher V_t), low V_DD and (for hold) fast/low-V_DD combinations; at near-threshold the exponential I–V makes ln D linear in ΔV_t, giving lognormal-like, right-skewed delay — consistent with the Boise thesis ("5%, may double or triple") and the LTAB moment extensions `[inference from V items]`.
- Consequently, "one local sigma for all corners" is wrong even if the Pelgrom coefficient A_VT is corner-independent; the interaction is captured operationally by characterizing LVF at every PVT corner with global parameters frozen at that corner (Key Question 4) `[inference]`.
- Independence is an *engineering assumption*, not a law: the nested-ANOVA/BPV framework makes cross-terms zero by construction, but (i) mismatch coefficients themselves vary lot-to-lot (A_VT is a process output), (ii) the patent evidence above shows practitioners have observed local-sigma/global-shift correlation, and (iii) partial N/P correlation and L–Vt coupling mean the "global" vector is multi-dimensional with a non-diagonal covariance — which is why foundries publish SSG/FFG (fully correlated N/P) *and* SSGNP/FFGNP (N/P-decorrelated, tighter) corners `[inference]`.
