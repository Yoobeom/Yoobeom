# Global (die-to-die) vs. local (within-die random) transistor variation at advanced nodes: measured magnitudes, corner/statistical-model definitions, and how the two are combined for timing margins

**Scope of this note (gap-filling pass).** The earlier pass found the decomposition methods (Stine/Boning 1997, Pelgrom 1989, Bowman 2002, BPV) but no numeric global-vs-local sigma data for FinFET/GAA nodes and no primary source on the correlation between the die-mean (global) shift and the within-die sigma (local). This note collects what could be found on: (1) measured σ_global vs σ_local numbers and A_VT trends across nodes (planar 28/45 nm → FinFET 22–5 nm → GAA), (2) dependence of local variation on the global corner / operating point, (3) how foundry statistical models define "total", "global (SSG/FFG)", "SSGNP/FFGNP" and "local mismatch MC" models, (4) whether within-die variation is still spatially correlated at advanced nodes, and (5) evidence on the pessimism ("double counting") of stacking a global corner with local sigma.

## Key Question 1 — Reported σ_global vs σ_local (Vth, Idsat, ring-oscillator/gate delay) at 22/16/14/10/7/5/3 nm, A_VT (Pelgrom) trends, FinFET quantization effects, GAA/nanosheet mismatch sources

### Takeaway
No public paper was reachable that tabulates σ_global next to σ_local for a production FinFET or GAA node; the only hard FinFET local number found is Intel's 14 nm single-fin random Vt σ of 19 mV (NMOS) / 24 mV (PMOS), normally distributed out to ±5σ (Giles et al., VLSI 2015, [SNIPPET]). For planar nodes the Berkeley 90/45 nm test-chip series gives the qualitative trend that random within-die variation more than doubled from 90 nm to 45 nm while systematic layout-dependent variation decreased, and ARM's 14 nm-era test structures found within-die variation to be spatially uncorrelated while die-to-die variation is strongly correlated but loses correlation as VDD is lowered (Yeric, ICMTS 2014, [SNIPPET]). Concrete, fully-read global-vs-local parameter sets exist only for open 130 nm PDKs (IHP SG13G2: global 1σ per PSP parameter, e.g. ±0.5 % on flat-band, ±1.33 % on oxide thickness, ±3.2 % on mobility; local Vt mismatch 3.9 mV·µm/√(WL) NMOS, 2.2 mV·µm/√(WL) PMOS per device; sky130: 3.36 mV·µm/√(WL) per device). GAA/nanosheet mismatch evidence is simulation-based (TCAD): metal-gate granularity (MGG) is reported as the dominant local source for both N3-class FinFET and nanosheet, LER/sidewall roughness contributes little to nanosheet Vt mismatch, and RDD contributes ~8 mV σVT in n-type FinFET/NW/NS structures.

### Inferences
- The available FinFET data point (Intel 14 nm, 19/24 mV single-fin σ_Vt,local) combined with the IHP/sky130 open-PDK structure suggests the standard practical decomposition at advanced nodes is: σ_local set by an area/fin-count-scaled Gaussian on Vt (and mobility, W, L), σ_global set by a per-parameter Gaussian on the die-mean model card; neither open PDK expresses any correlation between the two. [INFERENCE from PRIMARY-GH + SNIPPET]
- Converting per-device mismatch to Pelgrom's pair definition: σ(ΔVt,pair) = √2·σ(device), so the IHP NMOS per-device 3.9 mV·µm corresponds to A_VT(pair) ≈ 5.5 mV·µm and sky130's 3.36 mV·µm to ≈ 4.7 mV·µm at 130 nm — an order of magnitude above the sub-2 mV·µm regime expected for HKMG FinFET nodes, consistent with the FinFET-transition claims (Auth 2012, Natarajan 2014) but not itself a FinFET measurement. [INFERENCE; the FinFET A_VT regime is UNVERIFIED]
- Because RDD contributes ~8 mV and MGG is dominant in N3-class TCAD (Karner via secondary; Asenov group), the GAA local-mismatch budget is dominated by *gate-stack* granularity that does not scale with channel doping and only weakly with the die-mean Vt; this is the physical reason to expect weak coupling between the global corner and σ_local at GAA nodes (see KQ2). [INFERENCE]

## Key Question 2 — Does local variation depend on the global corner or operating point? (σ(delay) growth at slow corners / low VDD, LVF at SS vs TT, σ_Vt,local vs die-mean Vt, multiplicative D = D0(1+g)(1+l) models, "corner-dependent sigma")

### Takeaway
Every reachable source models the *parameter-level* local variation (ΔVt, Δβ, ΔW, ΔL) as independent of the global corner — the open IHP and sky130 PDKs literally include the same mismatch file under tt, ss and ff — while the *delay-level* local sigma is strongly corner- and voltage-dependent because the delay sensitivity to Vt grows toward exponential near threshold: Dreslinski et al. report performance variation from process alone rising ~5× from ~30 % at nominal VDD to ~150 % at 400 mV, a Synopsys patent states that at VDD ≤ 0.5 V the gate-delay σ becomes comparable to the nominal delay with a highly non-Gaussian PDF, and ARM's 14 nm-era measurements show die-to-die correlation itself degrading toward uncorrelated as VDD is lowered. Multiplicative combination is how STA tools actually stack the two: PrimeTime's effective derate is (AOCV/POCV local derate) × (guard-band scaling) + (additive margin) applied on top of a global-corner library, i.e. D ≈ D_corner·(1 + k·σ_local/μ), and an LVF library is characterized separately at each PVT corner so that σ_local(delay) is re-evaluated at SS/low-VDD rather than scaled from TT. No primary source was found that measures a correlation between a die's mean Vt shift and its within-die σ_Vt.

**C. How global corner and local sigma are combined (multiplicative stacking in STA)**
- PrimeTime derate composition: "For cells with AOCV models in PrimeTime: `<total derate> = <AOCV derate> * <flat aocvm_guardband scaling> + <flat add margin>`"; AOCV depth derate "f(n) = (3·sigma_n)/mean_n = ((3·sigma)/mean)·1/sqrt(n) = k·1/sqrt(n)" because "N-times such distribution is again a normal distribution with N-times scaled mean and sigma" (mean grows as n, σ as √n); example table `depth: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8` → `1.1 1.05 1.033 1.025 1.020 1.017 1.014 1.013`; "The global variation is countered running STA analysis at the corner conditions that form a bounding 'box' of where the real silicon is to operate", local variation means "some gates will be little faster, some little slower". ### Inferences
- Combining B and A: since foundry decks (at least the open ones) hold σ_Vt,local constant across corners, the LVF sigma difference between SS and TT libraries is produced by ∂D/∂Vt evaluated at the corner (larger at SS because of the lower overdrive), and by the lower VDD normally paired with the SS signoff corner; a first-order model is σ_D,local ≈ |∂D/∂Vt|_corner·σ_Vt,local/√N_fin, with ∂D/∂Vt ∝ D/(VDD − Vt)^α above threshold (α ≈ 1–2) and ∝ D/(n·kT/q) below threshold — this reproduces both the ~5× growth at 0.4 V (Dreslinski) and σ ≈ μ at ≤ 0.5 V (US 8,302,047). [INFERENCE]
- Yeric's observation (D2D correlation degrades at low VDD) is what a global+local model predicts if σ_D,local grows faster than σ_D,global as VDD drops: the *ratio* σ_local/σ_global of path delay is voltage dependent even with voltage-independent parameter sigmas. This argues for characterizing/validating the global-vs-local split per voltage corner rather than reusing a nominal-VDD ratio. [INFERENCE]
- Physically, planar RDF-limited σ_Vt does scale with the die-mean Vt and Tinv (Takeuchi-type σ_Vt ∝ √(Tinv·(Vt + 0.1 V)/(W·L)) [UNVERIFIED reconstruction of Takeuchi et al., IEDM 2007]), so at 28 nm bulk a slow (high-Vt, thick-Tinv) die should have a slightly larger σ_Vt,local; for undoped-channel FinFET/GAA where MGG/LER dominate (KQ1-D), this coupling should be weak. Neither statement was verifiable against a primary source this session. [INFERENCE/UNVERIFIED]

- No primary measurement of the correlation between a die's mean Vt (or RO frequency) shift and its within-die σ was found for any node; Kuhn TED 2011 and Yeric ICMTS 2014 are the most likely places to look.
- No foundry LVF documentation comparing σ tables at SS vs TT could be opened; the corner-dependence of LVF σ is supported only indirectly (characterization per corner + physics).
- No paper explicitly using a D = D0·(1 + g)·(1 + l) form with an interaction term was found; the PrimeTime derate composition is the closest documented multiplicative form.

## Key Question 3 — How foundry statistical compact models define "total" corners (SS/FF), "global" corners (SSG/FFG, N-sigma of the die-to-die distribution), decorrelated "SSGNP/FFGNP" corners, and local-mismatch Monte-Carlo models; documentation excerpts; BSIM-CMG/BPV FinFET extraction

### Takeaway
The clearest primary description of the modern hierarchy is TSMC's unified-variation patent (filed 2006, granted 2012): σ_total is the 3σ of all devices across ≥ 50 (preferably > 1000) dies, σ_global is the 3σ of the per-die medians, σ_local is obtained by subtraction (σ_local = √(σ_total² − σ_global²), or the reverse with σ_local from the average intra-die σ), and separate corner models are then generated for global variation (fixed corners) and local variation (Monte Carlo, from which pair mismatch is derived). Engineer notes reproducing TSMC/Cadence training material describe the resulting menu — "Total corner (TT/SS/FF/SF/FS)", "Global corner (TTG/SSG/FFG/SFG/FSG) + Local MC", "Local MC only", "Global MC + Local MC (Total MC)" — with the validation rule that the 3σ of a global-only MC must align with the global corner and the 3σ of global+local MC with the total corner, the definition "global corner = total corner minus the impact of local variation", and the SSGNP/FFGNP rule that when the N/P global correlation is weak (R² ≈ 0.15) the corner of an N/P-balanced circuit can be tightened from 3σ to ~2.5σ by N/P cancellation. The open IHP and sky130 PDKs implement the same three-layer structure (fixed ±3σ corners, global MC, area-scaled local MC).

### Cited Findings

**A. TSMC's unified global/local/total model (primary)**
- Bibliographic: Chung-Kai Lin, Cheng Hsiao, Sally Liu (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.), "Unified model for process variations in integrated circuits," US 8,275,584 B2, filed Dec. 12, 2006 (appl. 11/638,303), published as US 2008/0140363 A1, granted Sept. 25, 2012. [PRIMARY-PDF]: [official PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8275584.pdf); [Google Patents (not fetched)](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8275584B2/en).
- Conventional foundry practice described: "most of foundries provide the 'worst case' models, which assume that variations caused by different factors fluctuate in a same direction (corner)... Furthermore, it is necessary to provide a separated mismatch model that allows designers to assess their circuit robustness against local variations, which are usually performed by using the Monte Carlo simulation." The conventional flow "uses two independent flows for determining total variation corner and local variation mismatch models": total corners from identical test patterns on many dies; local sigma "defined from paired transistors mismatch data" following the 1/√(W·L) rule.
- Data collection: M dies (M > about 50, "more preferably greater than about one thousand"), N identical device groups per die (N ≥ about 15, preferably > 25); parameters "Vt_lin, Vt_sat, Id_lin, Id_sat, I_off, bulk threshold parameter Gamma, G_ds, G_m". "Standard deviation calculated from the intra-die data represents local variation, while standard deviation calculated for the inter-die data represents global variation."
- Global sigma from medians: per die the median of the N values is taken; "the median values tend to be substantially free from local variations"; "three times of the sigma [of the M medians] is defined as sigma_global" (or P·σ, P > 1, preferably > 2). Total sigma: "three time[s] of the standard deviation of the M·N data may be defined as ... sigma_total".
- Combination law: "sigma_total² = sigma_local² + sigma_global² + A [Eq. 1], wherein A is a coefficient factor. Further assuming that the coefficient factor A is negligible, there is: sigma_local = sqrt(sigma_total² − sigma_global²) [Eq. 2]". Second embodiment: σ_local = average over dies of the intra-die σ (N devices each), then "sigma_global = sqrt(sigma_total² − sigma_local²) [Eq. 3]". Claims 3, 5, 15, 22 recite these subtraction formulas.
- Corner models: "corner models can be generated for local variations (step 42) ... with the global variation sigma sigma_global and the collected inter-die data ... corner models for global variations"; "corners ... are furthest points that parameters are expected to deviate" on the parameter-space ellipse; "by using the corner models for local variations, local variations may be determined based on Monte Carlo simulation ... The mismatch variation of pair devices ... can then be determined based on local variations (step 50)"; global variations likewise by Monte Carlo (step 48). Result: "unify the global, local, total, and mismatch variations into a single model ... ensuring that sigma_total is always greater than sigma_local and sigma_global."
- Validation: FIG. 6 shows measured Idsat vs log(Ioff) ellipses (total = ellipse 70, local = ellipse 72, global inside 70); FIG. 7 plots 3σ of Idsat local variation vs a W/L-related parameter, simulated vs measured "regardless the scaling ... good matches".
*Contribution:* the primary, foundry-authored definition of σ_total/σ_global/σ_local, the subtraction (RSS) relation between them, the use of die medians to isolate the global term, and the generation of separate global corner and local Monte-Carlo models — which is exactly the SSG/FFG (global corner) + local-MC structure used at FinFET nodes.

## Key Question 4 — Is within-die systematic (spatial) variation still significant at FinFET nodes, or is local variation essentially random? Measured correlation lengths; relation to distance-based AOCV/LOCV derates

### Takeaway
The measured evidence trend is: at 90 → 45 nm planar the random within-die component more than doubled while systematic layout-dependent variation decreased and the residual WID spatial correlation was anisotropic and tied to poly density/orientation (Berkeley test chips); at the 14 nm generation ARM's dedicated test structures found within-die variation to be *spatially uncorrelated*, with the caution (paraphrased in the search summary) that layout/circuit-structure systematics can be misread as random-but-spatially-correlated variation. Patent literature from Synopsys and IBM documents that distance-dependent spatial correlation was measured and modelled (with correlation decreasing with separation) in the 65–45 nm era, and the analog-layout literature still models a local spatially-correlated term with a correlation distance of order 1 mm for capacitor arrays and finds that for correlation lengths ≥ ~1000 µm the total mismatch is dominated by the uncorrelated term; no paper reporting a measured within-die correlation length at 28 nm or below was reachable.

### Cited Findings
- ARM 14 nm-era test structures: "Measurement data from test chips indicate that within-die variation is spatially uncorrelated, and systematic effects such as layout pattern dependencies or circuit structure can be misinterpreted as random but spatially-correlated variation" [SNIPPET, second clause is the search engine's paraphrase]; the paper "presents a unified theory of process variation that includes inter-chip variation, intra-chip deterministic variation such as caused by proximity effects and metal density, and intra-chip statistical variation". — G. Yeric, "Processor yield at 14nm and beyond," ICMTS 2014: [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6841477); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269300085_Processor_yield_at_14nm_and_beyond). *Contribution:* the only advanced-node measured statement found; it supports treating WID as random + deterministic (layout-dependent) rather than as a spatially correlated random field.
- Berkeley 90 → 45 nm: "from 90 nm to 45 nm, random WID variation has more than doubled whereas systematic layout-dependent variations have decreased"; "the spatial correlation depends on the gate orientation and the direction of poly-Si spacing" (45 nm); "the WID apparent spatial correlation of ring oscillator frequency shows dependency on spacing direction" (90 nm) [SNIPPET]. — Pang & Nikolić, JSSC May 2009 (90 nm); Pang, Qian, Spanos, Nikolić, JSSC Aug. 2009 (45 nm), DOI 10.1109/JSSC.2009.2022217: [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5173763/). *Contribution:* measured trend that the spatially-correlated systematic part shrinks with scaling while the random part grows; apparent correlation is layout-induced.
- Synopsys (Luo, Sinha, Su, Chiang), "Predicting IC manufacturing yield by considering both systematic and random intra-die process variations," US 8,000,826 B2 (granted Aug. 16, 2011): "random intra-die variations tend to be spatially correlated. ### Inferences
- The distance-based AOCV/LOCV derate tables in Liberty (`ocv_derate_distance_group`, covered in the sibling LVF note) encode exactly the IBM/Synopsys-era distance-decaying correlation; the Yeric 14 nm finding (WID spatially uncorrelated) implies the *distance* dimension of AOCV carries little information at FinFET nodes, which is consistent with the industry's move from AOCV (depth+distance) to POCV/LVF (per-cell sigma, depth-only √N averaging) at 16 nm and below. ## Key Question 5 — Quantifying the pessimism ("double counting") of stacking a global corner with local sigma; corner-plus-OCV vs total-sigma comparisons

### Takeaway
TSMC's own patent gives the clearest primary statement of the double-counting mechanism — applying local variation at the corners of the *total* distribution predicts variation "greater than the total variation", and "sigma_local should always be no greater than sigma_total" — and shows (FIG. 6, Idsat vs Ioff) that the unified σ_total² = σ_global² + σ_local² model "substantially eliminated" it; the fix is precisely the global-corner (SSG) + local-MC/LVF split. The engineer-transcribed corner rules quantify two consequences: cell delay orders as SSGNP < SSG < SS (FFGNP > FFG > FF) and the N/P-decorrelated corner is ~2.5σ instead of 3σ. A worked scalar example (below, my derivation) shows that with σ_G = σ_L the corner-plus-3σ-local stack is 3(σ_G + σ_L) = 6σ versus the true 3√(σ_G² + σ_L²) = 4.24σ, i.e. ~41 % excess margin on the variation term; the excess falls to ~27 % when σ_L = 0.33 σ_G and ~9 % when σ_L = 0.1 σ_G, so the size of the pessimism is governed by the σ_L/σ_G ratio — the very number that is missing for FinFET/GAA nodes (KQ1 gap). No published silicon-validated percentage for corner+OCV vs total-σ at a FinFET node was reachable.

### Cited Findings
- TSMC, US 8,275,584 B2 [PRIMARY-PDF]: "in the existing corner models, it is assumed that all devices are sampled at a same process corner, ignoring the fact that differences may arise between devices due to over-chip variations ... This results in an over-pessimistic design. ... Without proper description of global and local variations, designers may have to apply the local variations on the corners of total variations, resulting in a double counting of local variations due to the fact that total variations already include local variations." FIG. 2: "Ellipses 22 are local variations (sigma_local) applied at corners 24 of the total variation. It is noted that the predicted variations (within an outer boundary of the shaded area) may be greater than the total variation ... The shadowed area indicates the extra design margin that will be wasted." "sigma_local is a portion of the sigma_total. Therefore, sigma_local should always be no greater than sigma_total. However, existing models may provide greater sigma_local than sigma_total. One of the reasons ... is that 1/sqrt(W·L) is used as sigma_local." FIG. 6 (measured Idsat/Ioff): "Compared to conventional modeling, it is noted that the double counting of local variations has been substantially eliminated. Accordingly, ... over-design ... is at least reduced, and possibly substantially eliminated." [official PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8275584.pdf). *Contribution:* primary, foundry-authored identification of corner+mismatch double counting, its geometric interpretation (local ellipses hung on total-corner points exceed the total ellipse), and the RSS relation that removes it.

[PVTCorner.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CaseyZhu/my_git_hub/7232a669a64df31377f70d0ef0268daf3c5f8fe4/PVTCorner.txt); [raytroop corners.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raytroop/raytroop.github.io/98a66e32ed9f79ab3ac3659341045162c7823256/source/_posts/corners.md). *Contribution:* ordinal (delay) and one numeric (2.5σ vs 3σ) statement of the pessimism removed by successive decorrelation.

### Inferences
- (Worked quantification — my derivation, not from a source.) Let the path delay be D = D0·(1 + g + l) with g ~ N(0, σ_G²) common to the die and l ~ N(0, σ_L²/n_eff) for a path of n_eff independent stages (σ_L per stage). True 3σ upper bound: D0·(1 + 3√(σ_G² + σ_L²/n_eff)). Corner+local stack: D0·(1 + 3σ_G + 3σ_L/√n_eff). Excess = 3(σ_G + σ_L') − 3√(σ_G² + σ_L'²) with σ_L' = σ_L/√n_eff. For σ_L' = σ_G: 6σ vs 4.24σ (41 % excess of the variation term); for σ_L' = 0.5σ_G: 4.5σ vs 3.35σ (34 %); for σ_L' = 0.33σ_G: 4.0σ vs 3.16σ (27 %); for σ_L' = 0.1σ_G: 3.3σ vs 3.015σ (9 %). In delay terms the excess is (3σ_G + 3σ_L' − 3√(σ_G²+σ_L'²))·D0. [INFERENCE]
- If instead the SS *total* corner (already containing local) is used with LVF on top, the stack is 3√(σ_G²+σ_L²) + 3σ_L' — the TSMC FIG. 2 case — whose excess over the true bound is 3σ_L' regardless of σ_G. [INFERENCE]
- The secondary claim "3σ_lo ≪ 3σ_go" (CSDN) and Giles' 19–24 mV single-fin σ_Vt suggest that at FinFET nodes the per-path σ_L' after √n averaging is small relative to σ_G for deep paths, so the double-counting pessimism on *long* paths is modest (~10 % of the variation term or less), while on short paths (small n_eff, e.g. clock-to-Q + one gate, hold checks) it can approach the 30–40 % regime; this is why the pessimism is felt most in hold/short-path closure. [INFERENCE — depends on the unmeasured σ_L/σ_G ratio]
