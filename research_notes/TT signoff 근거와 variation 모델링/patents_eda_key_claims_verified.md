## Key question 1 — Which Synopsys documents (own filings plus the Extreme DA team's SCS-OCV/POCV family) claim parametric/moment-based OCV timing, and what exactly do their independent claims recite?

### Takeaway
The Synopsys line runs from the Extreme DA team's SCS-OCV family (priority Aug. 2010; per-arc nominal + sigma, N-sigma-preserving statistical max, N-sigma corner slack apportioning) to a 2013-priority "generalized moment" patent (skewness and higher moments per gate), a 2018-priority moment-algebra patent (conjugation/negation of moment sets for slack), and a 2016-2020 family on POCV delay/transition-time variation propagation through wires (Elmore-based); none of the claims read on the SCS-OCV/POCV documents recites a nominal/typical corner plus a global statistical margin — global (chip-to-chip) variation is explicitly left to corners in the descriptions.

### Inferences
- The SCS-OCV claims are the closest Synopsys claim language to "typical corner + statistical margin": every arc carries a nominal value plus a sigma for local random variation, and the N-sigma corner is reconstructed from the propagated (nominal, sigma) pair ("preserves N sigma corner delay values"; "delay shift value ... under which a corner slack is reached"). Global variation is described as chip-to-chip variation handled by corners, so the global-corner-plus-local-sigma split is present in the description but only the local-sigma side is claimed.
- The "moment" lineage (9,183,333 → 10,073,934/10,275,554 [Cadence] → 10,755,023) tracks the Liberty LVF "moments" extension (mean shift, std dev, skewness): Synopsys claims per-gate higher-order moments in 2013 and moment algebra for slack in 2018.
- Because 7,992,114 originated at Magma (2008) and was continued by Synopsys after the 2012 acquisition, its "select a margin that satisfies the timing requirement after adjusting for random (location-independent) and systematic (spatially correlated) effects" claim is the earliest EDA-vendor claim found that explicitly recites a design-specific OCV margin derived from separate random and systematic components.

### Gaps
- Inventors of US 9,183,333 and the earliest filing/priority date of the US 10,255,395 → 10,783,301 → 11,288,426 family could not be confirmed (search budget exhausted; HTML sites blocked).
- Claim 1 of US 8,843,864 was not retrievable (no PDF in the legacy store, no pre-grant publication number found).
- No Synopsys patent was found whose claims recite scaling global variation to a 1-sigma level in a library or a "typical corner plus global sigma margin" signoff; the earlier pass's hypothesis remains unconfirmed.
- US 9,710,594 B2 "Variation-aware timing analysis using waveform construction" (snippet: voltage waveforms at a first component's output under processing conditions are stored and re-used at a second component) has no confirmed assignee or inventors — **unverified**.

## Key question 2 — Which Cadence documents claim SOCV-style timing, statistical/variation-aware library characterization (Altos-like), LVF moments, and Monte-Carlo corner extraction?

### Takeaway
Cadence's verified claims cover (a) predicting circuit performance at a user-chosen confidence level from two or more corner libraries (2004 priority, granted 2014), (b) sensitivity-based within-die characterization that annotates variation sensitivities into the cell library (2008), (c) waveform-based variational STA ("WAVSTAN", 2008 priority), (d) post-2015 LVF-moment SSTA (skewness/coskewness tables, 10,073,934 / 10,275,554) and adaptive-sensitivity characterization of "sigma and moments" (10,789,406, 2018), and (e) K-sigma / worst-sample corner extraction from Monte Carlo (9,805,158 / 9,836,564, 2015) — the last two being Cadence, not Solido, filings.

### Inferences
- Cadence's claimed route to a "statistical corner" is numerical rather than analytical: interpolate between existing corner libraries to a user confidence level (8,645,881), or extract a K-sigma corner/worst sample from Monte Carlo (9,805,158 / 9,836,564). - No Cadence claim found recites a global/chip-mean variation term separately from local sigma; 10,235,484 (Cadence, "Automatic timing-sensitive circuit extraction") describes global = chip mean and local = mismatch in its text, but it is a circuit-extraction patent, not a timing-margin one — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10235484B2/en).

## Key question 3 — Which IBM documents claim statistical corners, hybrid corner+statistical analysis, corner projection, or corner/margin selection from SSTA?

### Takeaway
IBM's post-2007 claims form a coherent "corners from statistics" toolkit: RSS sensitivity credit for independent parameters on top of a deterministic worst corner (7,555,740), projecting a single SSTA run to k selected corners (8,141,012), a single run with non-separable deterministic-corner and statistical parameters that is later projected to a corner (2012/0117527), a "statistical single worst-case Liberty library" whose delay/power values are WC statistical corners (8,413,095), shift/scale of canonical random variables with explicit global/spatial/random sensitivities (8,806,402 / 8,949,765), and, post-2015, selecting corners and margins for a coverage target from an SSTA-derived parameterized model (9,501,609 family) plus term-collapsing for efficiency (10,055,532).
