# TT (typical-corner) timing signoff with a statistical global+local margin: technical basis, tool support, and sources

Research date: 2026-09-30. Scope: digital STA signoff at advanced CMOS nodes (28 nm to 3 nm), with foundational SSTA work where it establishes the argument.

**How to read the evidence labels.** Web access in this session was heavily restricted (nearly every publisher, vendor, patent and forum domain was blocked by the egress proxy, and the search budget was exhausted). Three evidence classes are therefore distinguished throughout:

- **[DOC]** = quoted from an official tool/standard document whose full text was read in this session (PrimeTime User Guide, PrimeTime Variables and Commands references, Liberty User Guide 2017.06, Innovus/Tempus Text Command Reference and Innovus 25.10 man pages, OpenSTA documentation, all obtained as Markdown/text mirrors on GitHub; the mirror repositories are cited, and the content should be re-checked against the vendor originals before being quoted externally).
- **[SCRIPT]** = an exact command/variable name observed in real production or academic flow scripts / tool logs on GitHub (verifies the spelling and typical values, not the semantics).
- **[SNIPPET]** = a fact taken from a search-result excerpt of a page whose full text could NOT be fetched. Treat as "reported by the source", not fully verified.
- **[INFERENCE]** = my own derivation or synthesis; not attributable to a source.

## Key Question 1: What is the formal statistical argument for TT signoff (variance addition, 3σ double counting, path-depth scaling, common-mode cancellation)?

### Takeaway
No public paper was found that lays out "TT + total statistical margin" signoff end-to-end, but every building block of the argument is documented: (a) corner signoff fixes every *global* parameter at its ±3σ extreme and then adds *local* variation on top by an early/late split or by sigma-based OCV [SNIPPET: Tetelbaum 2014]; (b) EDA vendors and the Liberty standard model local variation as per-cell, statistically independent random variables whose path contribution grows as √N while global/correlated variation grows linearly with N [SNIPPET: EDN POCV article; DOC: PrimeTime User Guide]; (c) therefore stacking "3σ global corner" and "3σ local" is a linear sum of two things that physically add in quadrature, which is where the pessimism comes from [INFERENCE supported by RSS tolerance analysis and an SRAM-corner patent that explicitly contrasts "sum" vs "root-mean-square" global/local corners].

### Cited Findings

**How corner signoff treats global vs local today**
- "Every global variation is set to its three-standard deviation (3 sigma) extreme corners, one corner providing the fastest signal propagation checked in the fast chip timing analysis and another corner providing the slowest signal propagation checked in the slow chip timing analysis." Local variations "(also referred to as on-chip variations) are modeled by creating timing skew through an early/late split, making early path latency smaller and late path latency larger." — [SNIPPET] A. Tetelbaum, "Corner-based Timing Signoff and What Is Next," AnySilicon white paper, Jan 2014, [PDF](https://anysilicon.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Corner_based_Signoff_paper_Jan_2014a.pdf) (HTML version: [anysilicon.com](https://anysilicon.com/corner-based-timing-signoff-next/)). The same paper counts corners as "5 standard process corners (SS, SF, FF, FS, TT) x 2 temperature points x 4 metal points x 4 voltage points = 5x2x4x4 = 160 corners" and states that FinFET/DPT effects "may produce extreme delays at intermediate points, requiring additional corners."
- "The SSG 'global corner' includes only the global variation (leaving on-die variation to path structure-aware AOCV, bounding box diagonal) of clock and data circuit elements." "SSG (slow global), TTG (typical global) and FFG (fast global) only include between wafer variance." "For STA, global corners are the basis for all of the advanced derates: AOCV, POCV, SOCV, Liberty Variance Format (LVF)." — [SNIPPET] A. B. Kahng, "New Game, New Goal Posts: A Recent History of Timing Closure," invited paper (DAC 2015; venue from memory, not re-verified), [PDF](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/330/c330.pdf); the "between wafer variance" sentence and the "basis for all advanced derates" sentence surfaced in the same search cluster from [Purl Silicon, "STEP 9 PVT Corners and Variations"](https://purlsilicon.com/step-9-pvt-corners-and-variations/), [deepchip 0535-01 "CLKDA Isadore warns..."](https://www.deepchip.com/items/0535-01.html) and ["Timing analysis journey from OCV to LVF," J. **Local variation averages (√N); global/correlated variation adds linearly (N)**
- "POCV distinguishes between correlated global variations, which grow linearly with path length, and uncorrelated local variations, which grow with the square root of path length." — [SNIPPET] "Parametric on-chip variation: A step towards accurate timing analysis," EDN ([edn.com](https://www.edn.com/parametric-on-chip-variation-a-step-towards-accurate-timing-analysis/), [ednasia.com](https://www.ednasia.com/parametric-on-chip-variation-a-step-towards-accurate-timing-analysis/)).
- "A longer path that has more gates tends to have less total variation because the random variations from gate to gate cancel each other out. A path that spans a larger physical distance across the chip tends to have larger systematic variation." — [DOC] Synopsys PrimeTime User Guide, Chapter 13 "Variation", section "Advanced On-Chip Variation" (Markdown mirror in [assrs/eda, pt/PrimeTime-User-Guide.md](https://github.com/assrs/eda)).
- POCV single-parameter model: "The timing behavior of each cell is considered statistically independent. As arrivals and slews propagate through the design logic, each cell's delay and slew response is statistically combined with the incoming arrival and slews to compute the outgoing arrivals and slews." — [DOC] PrimeTime User Guide, "Single-Parameter Random Variation" (same mirror).
- The AOCV depth formula that quantifies the √N averaging: `f(n) = (3 × sigma_n) / mean_n = ((3 × sigma) / mean) × 1/sqrt(n) = k × 1/sqrt(n)`, i.e. the relative 3σ spread of an n-stage path of identical, independent stages shrinks as 1/√n. — [DOC-like, fetched] brabect1, "OCV and timing derating #sta" (GitHub gist referencing PrimeTime S-2021.06-SP5 and Tempus 21.14), [gist](https://gist.github.com/brabect1/6281f4cf9fb53002fb17f15fa3bf4f62).
- "Local variations aren't correlated, which means you don't accumulate the variations by adding them; instead, you sum the squares of the variations and then take the square root. SSTA uses a mean and a deviation to represent delays instead of a single number." and "For an inverter at normal threshold and supply, the 1-sigma value of relative delay variation due to local variation is ~5%, and depending on the supply and threshold, it may double or triple. If you use 3-sigma local variation, its impact on the path delay will be ~8% to 3% depending on the length of the path." — [SNIPPET] Semiconductor Engineering, "Process Variation Not A Solved Issue," [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/process-variation-not-a-solved-issue/).
- Worked PrimeTime example of quadrature accumulation: in the `report_timing -variation` sample, incremental sigmas ("Sensit") of 3.00 and 2.00 accumulate to a path Sensit of 3.61 (= √(3²+2²) = 3.606), while means add linearly (1.00 + 1.00 = 2.00); the corner value is `Mean ± K*Sensit` with K = `timing_pocvm_report_sigma`. — [DOC] PrimeTime User Guide, "Reporting the POCV Analysis Results".
- "DSTA (Deterministic Static Timing Analysis) corners are pessimistic because real path variation is not a linear sum of cell variation." — [SNIPPET] appears in the result cluster containing B. Bautz and S. Lokanadham (Cadence), "SOCV," TAU Workshop 2014 slides, [PDF](http://www.tauworkshop.com/2014/Slides/Bautz_SOCV_TAU_2014.pdf) (attribution to this deck not fully confirmed).

**Global + local combine in quadrature; a "sum" corner is more conservative than an RSS corner**
- Root-sum-square (RSS) tolerance rule: "assumes all dimensions follow normal distributions, all processes are independent, and we're calculating 3-sigma (99.73%) limits using the formula: σ_total = √(σ₁² + σ₂² + σ₃² + ... + σₙ²)." — [SNIPPET] Blackrock Engineering, "Statistical Tolerance Analysis: RSS and Monte Carlo Methods," [blog](https://www.blackrock-engineering.ca/blog/statistical-tolerance-analysis-rss-monte-carlo/).
- An SRAM worst-case-corner patent contrasts exactly the two ways of combining global and local variation: "A G corner represents the single cell 3 sigma Iread limit from chip mean Monte Carlo simulations, while a performance (P) corner represents the single cell 3 sigma Iread limit from Monte Carlo simulations varying parameters both chip to chip and within chip and taking a root mean square sum of global and local variations." ... "The SRM corner, which is more conservative from the global and local components being added as a sum, therefore allows for a greater product yield." — [SNIPPET] US Patent 8,352,895, "Model library implementation and methodology for worst case performance modeling for SRAM cells," [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8352895).
- "Each chip has a local distribution on top of the global distribution due to local variations, and not all parts within a [+3σ, −3σ] window will yield above target due to local variations." — [SNIPPET] B. Klass, "Design for Yield Using Statistical Design," Stanford EE380 lecture, 7 Feb 2007, [PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee380/Abstracts/070207-EE380_Design_for_Yield_Klass_1p0.pdf).
- "Traditional Monte Carlo Analysis typically verifies a 3-sigma yield. Since 3-sigma corresponds to only a 0.13% probability, verification requires a large sample size." — [SNIPPET] Cadence white paper, "Accelerating Monte Carlo Analysis at Advanced Nodes," [PDF](https://www.cadence.com/content/dam/cadence-www/global/en_US/documents/tools/custom-ic-analog-rf-design/monte-carlo-analysis-at-advanced-nodes-wp.pdf).
- "Combined local and global process variation for metrics can be calculated based on global and local distributions, and where FF, TT, and SS libraries are available for voltage and temperature corners, interpolation-based techniques may be used." — [SNIPPET] US Patent 12,430,486, "Combined global and local process variation modeling," [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12430486) (assignee not verified).
- "One approach minimizes double counting of embedded within-die-variation. An imaginary variation component was introduced mainly to cancel the double counted variation." — [SNIPPET] surfaced among US 8,843,864 / US 8,555,222 "Statistical corner evaluation for complex on-chip variation model" ([8843864](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8843864), [8555222](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8555222)) and US 8,413,095 "Statistical single library including on chip variation for rapid timing and power analysis" ([USPTO](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8413095)); the exact patent containing the sentence could not be confirmed, but it documents that "double counting" of within-die variation embedded in corner libraries is a recognized problem addressed by patents.

**Foundational SSTA formulation (global sources + independent random term)**
- C. Visweswariah, K. Ravindran, K. Kalafala, S. G. Walker, S. Narayan, "First-Order Incremental Block-Based Statistical Timing Analysis," DAC 2004 (later IEEE TCAD 2006) — the canonical first-order delay model expresses each delay as a nominal value plus linear sensitivities to a set of *global* (correlated) sources of variation plus an *independent random* term; this is the formal basis for "global adds linearly along a path, random adds in quadrature" [INFERENCE about content; paper existence verified via search: mirror [PDF](https://algos.inesc-id.pt/projects/timewarp/ref16.pdf); full text not fetched]. The same group's DAC 2003 paper "Statistical timing for parametric yield prediction of digital integrated circuits" (J. A. G. Jess, K. Kalafala, S. R. Naidu, R. H. J. M. Otten, C. Visweswariah) "proposes three novel path-based algorithms for statistical timing analysis and parametric yield prediction ... implemented in the context of the EinsTimer static timing analyzer"; "Because of variability, only a fraction of all manufactured circuits can meet the required timing constraints. This fraction is called the parametric yield." — [SNIPPET] [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/775832.776066), mirrors at [pure.tue.nl](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/2250330/Metis202401.pdf) and [Berkeley](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~alanmi/research/timing/papers/jess.pdf).
- IBM's EinsStat "has incremental statistical timing analysis capability, including support for front-end, back-end and environmental variations. This solution allows efficient coverage of the entire process space in a single static timing analysis with reduced timing pessimism and includes support for several diagnostics including the sensitivity of all timing quantities to process variables." — [SNIPPET] EE Times, "IBM markets statistical timing analyzer," [eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/ibm-markets-statistical-timing-analyzer/).

**Common-mode (launch vs. capture) behaviour of correlated variation**
- CPPR rationale: "the portion of the clock tree shared between the launch and capture paths is physically the same silicon, so it cannot be simultaneously fast and slow." and "If a buffer comes in both the path of launch and capture then statistically it's nearly impossible that during launch it possesses variation on one extreme and during capture the variation goes to the exact opposite." — [SNIPPET] ecrionix, "Day 10: Clock Tree Analysis — Skew, Jitter, Latency, CPPR," [ecrionix.org](https://ecrionix.org/sta/day-10-clock-analysis/); VLSI System Design, "Common clock path pessimism removal (CPPR) - Part 4," [vlsisystemdesign.com](https://www.vlsisystemdesign.com/common-clock-path-pessimism-removal-cppr-part-4/).
- How a deterministic OCV corner enforces the anti-correlated worst case: "In on-chip variation mode, the tool applies worst-case delay adjustments (both early and late) at the same time. For example, for a setup check, it applies late derating on the launch clock path and data path, and early derating on the capture clock path. For a hold check, it does the opposite." — [DOC] PrimeTime Tool Commands reference, `set_timing_derate` (mirror in assrs/eda, pt/PrimeTime-Suite-Tool-Commands.md).
- Global process "mistracking" is the residual that does NOT cancel: a signoff patent lists "transistor threshold voltage mistracking, transistor doping mistracking, transistor channel length mistracking, metal layer resistivity mistracking, metal layer track width mistracking, and metal layer thickness mistracking" and states the need "to reduce timing pessimism during static timing analysis (STA) so that IC designs can achieve safe timing signoff and operate at higher clock speeds"; it also defines "The TT corner corresponds to semiconductor manufacturing conditions that cause NMOS and PMOS transistors to switch at a typical speed" and notes "Timing signoff may be performed at a few corners, e.g., at the fast-fast (FF) corner, typical-typical (TT) corner, and the slow-slow (SS) corner." — [SNIPPET] US Patent 11,893,332 B2, "Global mistracking analysis in integrated circuit design," [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11893332B2/en), [USPTO PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11893332) (assignee/inventors not verified).
- Tempus models exactly this residual as a separate "VT skew" derate: `timing_enable_vtskew_derate_mode` — "Due to variations in the manufacturing process, the voltage threshold (Vth) of a given Vth class (e.g., SVT, LVT, ULVT) can show some variations that might result in cells of that class operating either faster or slower than the nominal value. The Tempus Vth skew analysis automates the process of selecting which Vth permutations need to be run"; `set_vt_skew_derate -threshold_voltage_group SVT -fast 0.90 -slow 1.10` with `-mean` / `-sigma` selecting the SOCV component the derate applies to. — [DOC] Innovus Text Command Reference (legacy) mirror in assrs/eda.
- A practitioner description of the same effect under PrimeTime: "MixedVt / MixedLg derates: ... process is not correlated for different VT, so LVT might be at SS −3σ corner, but ULVT instead of being perfectly at SS −3σ corner, it may be a little bit faster or slower ... OCV models local process variations, while Mixed VT is modeling global process corner correlation. We model this MixedVt correlation effect via derate." — [fetched blog] "PrimeTime and Timing Derate" (2022-05-07), [chipgun/nibaix.github.io](https://github.com/chipgun/nibaix.github.io/blob/master/2022/05/07/PrimeTime-and-OCV/index.html).

### Inferences
- **Variance bookkeeping.** For a path of N stages with per-stage global sensitivity g_i (fully correlated across the die) and per-stage local sigma s_i (independent): σ_path² = (Σ g_i·σ_G)² + Σ s_i². With identical stages: σ_path = √(N²·g²σ_G² + N·s²) = N·g·σ_G·√(1 + s²/(N g² σ_G²)). The global term is O(N), the local term O(√N); this is precisely the "linear vs. square-root growth" the EDN POCV article and the PrimeTime AOCV chapter describe. At a fixed corner the tool sees only the local term (LVF sigma) and freezes the global term at its 3σ value.
- **Where the double-count comes from.** Corner+LVF signoff evaluates delay at μ + 3σ_G·N·g + K·√(Σ s_i²), i.e. it linearly adds a 3σ global excursion and a K-σ local excursion. The true K-σ point of the combined distribution is μ + K·√((N g σ_G)² + Σ s_i²). For equal contributions (σ_G,path = σ_L,path = σ) the corner number is 6σ versus 4.24σ (=3√2 σ) for the true 3σ total — a 41 % over-margin on the variation term; for σ_G,path = 2σ_L,path the corner gives 9σ_L vs 6.7σ_L (+34 %). This is the quantitative "3σ_g + 3σ_l vs 3√(σ_g² + σ_l²)" argument in the question; it is an inference from the RSS rule and the SRAM patent's "sum vs RMS" corners, not a statement found in an STA paper.
- **Why typical.** If the analysis is run at TT (μ of the global distribution) and the margin is K·√(σ_G,path² + σ_L,path²), then (i) the global part is applied at its correct statistical weight instead of a hard 3σ, (ii) the local part still uses LVF, and (iii) setup and hold can use different K. The catch is that σ_G,path must come from somewhere: either from global-sensitivity-aware libraries/parameters (IBM-style SSTA with global sources), or from characterizing LVF-style "total" sigmas at TT that include global spread (the "statistical single library" and "combined global and local process variation modeling" patents describe exactly such libraries), or from a derate that represents the global spread (Tempus `set_socv_rc_variation_factor` literally "applies global variation as percentage of the nominal delay" for interconnect).
- **Common-mode credit.** A global shift (e.g., all NMOS slow) moves launch clock, data and capture clock together; for setup the residual is (data + launch − capture) sensitivity, which is proportional to the *difference* in global sensitivity between the data path and the capture clock path, not the sum. A hard SS corner cannot exploit this except through CRPR on the physically common clock segment; a global-parameter-aware analysis (IBM canonical model) gets it automatically. What remains is inter-parameter mistracking (Vt-class, N/P, FEOL/BEOL), which is why patents and Tempus provide explicit "mistracking"/"VT skew" derates even in statistical flows.
- **Sigma levels.** All three signoff tools default to K = 3 (`timing_pocvm_corner_sigma` 3, `timing_nsigma_multiplier` 3.0, OpenSTA `sta_pocv_quantile` 3). K is a per-path (per-endpoint) quantile, not a chip-yield number: at 3σ, 0.13 % of paths exceed the corner (Cadence MC white paper); a design with many near-critical endpoints needs a higher K or a yield-based interpretation (IBM's "parametric yield" framing).

## Key Question 2: Under what names is the concept described in the literature and industry?

### Takeaway
The vocabulary is fragmented: the industry says "global corner" (SSG/TTG/FFG) + "local/on-chip variation" (OCV/AOCV/POCV/SOCV/LVF); the research/IDM side says "statistical timing with global parameters", "projection to corners", "parametric yield", "hybrid multi-corner statistical STA"; patents say "statistical corner", "combined global and local process variation", "global mistracking"; the phrase "signoff at typical" appears (Kahng) but no paper titled "TT signoff" was found.

### Inferences
- The practical industry realisation of "TT signoff" today is not a single named methodology but the combination (i) foundry *global* corners that by construction exclude on-die variation, (ii) LVF/POCV/SOCV for local variation, and (iii) either tightening the global corner (Kahng's "tightened corners") or moving it to TTG plus an explicit global margin (patent/IBM-style). Searching under "global corner + LVF", "tightened corner", "statistical corner", "hybrid multi-corner SSTA" and "parametric yield" finds more than searching for "TT signoff".

- No English or Korean source using the literal phrases "TT signoff", "typical corner signoff" or "total variation corner" as a methodology name was found (search budget exhausted before Korean-language queries could be run).

## Key Question 3: What EDA tool support exists (PrimeTime POCV/LVF, Tempus SOCV, Liberty attributes, OpenSTA), with exact commands?

### Takeaway
Both signoff tools implement the *local* half of the argument natively (per-arc mean/sigma from LVF, quadrature propagation, K-sigma corners, moment-based non-Gaussian support, statistical constraints/slews, via/RC variation) but neither documents a "global variation parameter" mode; global variation is still handled by the library corner plus scalar derates/guardbands (PrimeTime `-pocvm_guardband`, Tempus `set_socv_rc_variation_factor`, `set_vt_skew_derate`). - Characterization cost: LVF "theoretically improves accuracy but requires extensive characterization for different cells, threshold voltages, strengths, and PVT corners." — [SNIPPET] (Cadence 16nm white paper / Semiconductor Engineering "Model Variation And Its Impact On Cell Characterization," [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/model-variation-and-its-impact-on-cell-characterization/)).

### Inferences
- Neither PrimeTime nor Tempus exposes a documented "global process parameter" (die-to-die) statistical mode analogous to IBM's canonical form; both treat cells as *independent* random variables (PrimeTime: "each cell instance is considered to be statistically independent"; Tempus distinguishes "random cell" vs "correlated" components only for slew-delay correlation). Therefore a TT-signoff flow in these tools must inject the global margin externally: (a) through the library corner choice (TTG vs SSG), (b) through mean/sigma guardbands (`-pocvm_guardband`, `-pocvm_coefficient_scale_factor`, `set_socv_rc_variation_factor`, `set_vt_skew_derate`), or (c) by characterizing "total" LVF sigmas at TT that already include the global spread (which then loses the launch/capture common-mode credit because the tool treats them as independent).
- Tempus' default `mean_and_three_sigma_bounded` merge rule and PrimeTime's "statistical graph pessimism" adjustment show that graph-based statistical max() is itself a source of bounded pessimism; PBA removes it.

### Gaps
- Official Synopsys/Cadence documentation on any "global variation"/"statistical global corner" feature was not found; the names `timing_pocvm_precision_mode`, `timing_ocvm_precision_mode_value`, `timing_pocvm_enable_distribution_report` from the task prompt could not be verified and may not exist.
- Descriptions of Tempus `delaycal_wire_corner_sigma_multiplier`, `delaycal_via_corner_sigma_multiplier`, `delaycal_wire_var_reporting_sigma_multiplier` were not extracted (only their existence).
- Siemens/Mentor timing tools: no material found.

## Key Question 5: What are the known pitfalls (non-Gaussian low-V behaviour, slew/constraint variation, sigma vs corner interaction, RC corners, temperature inversion, IR drop, aging) and how does statistical signoff handle them?

### Takeaway
Documented pitfalls are: non-Gaussian (skewed) delay distributions at ultra-low VDD (handled by moment-based LVF and tool "extended moments"/"accuracy_mode high/ultra"); slew-induced correlation between stages (delay/slew correlation options); constraint (setup/hold) sigma being symmetric and library-"folded"; graph-merging pessimism; interconnect variation (via/RC variation as a separate statistical or global-percentage term); and global mistracking (Vt-class, N/P, BEOL) that a single corner or independent-cell model cannot see. Temperature inversion and aging are handled only through corners/derates, not statistically, in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **Non-Gaussian at low voltage.** "For more accuracy in advanced process nodes and very low supply voltages, timing behaviors can be modeled as asymmetric (non-Gaussian) distributions ... Because a moment-based distribution is asymmetrical, its statistical mean is no longer equal to its no-variation (nominal) value ... In variation reports, mean values are the mean-shifted (away from nominal) values." "If normal POCV cell models are used in a moment-based analysis, they are converted to moment-based models according to their minimum (early) and maximum (late) variation behaviors." — [DOC] PrimeTime User Guide. "Non-Gaussian timing variation is more prevalent at ultra-low voltage and poses a bigger impact on design robustness and yield"; "Mean-shift and skewness modeling are essential at ultra-low VDDs." — [SNIPPET] (LVF moments press release / ULV LVF paper cluster). "The Liberty Variation Format loses accuracy when the timing distribution is non-Gaussian due to growing process variations." — [SNIPPET] "LVF2: A statistical timing model based on Gaussian mixture ...," DAC 2024 ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3649329.3655670), [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/221555/1/3649329.3655670.pdf)). P. Ghanta (Synopsys), "Importance of Modeling Non-Gaussianities in STA in sub-16nm Nodes," TAU Workshop 2016 ([PDF](http://www.tauworkshop.com/2016/slides/10_TAU2016_Ghanta_nonGaussian_POCV.pdf)) [title only]. Tempus: `delaycal_socv_accuracy_mode high` "Considers skewness of non-gaussian distribution ... local to one stage only"; `ultra` "also propagates skewness to the next stage in PBA analysis" [DOC].
- **Local sigma grows at low V / with Vt.** "the 1-sigma value of relative delay variation due to local variation is ~5%, and depending on the supply and threshold, it may double or triple" [SNIPPET, semiengineering]; "Lower VDD ... increases sensitivity to delay, transition and constraint variation (as Vdd is close to Vth, so small change in Vdd causes large changes in current)" [fetched blog, chipgun]. [INFERENCE] Because LVF sigma is characterized per PVT corner, the local sigma used with an SS/low-V corner is larger than the TT sigma; a TT-signoff flow that reuses TT-characterized sigma to cover the SS tail under-estimates local variation at the slow tail unless the global margin is inflated or sigma is taken from the slow-corner LVF.
- **Slew and delay correlation across stages.** "In a basic POCV analysis, each cell instance is considered to be statistically independent. However, when slew variation is enabled, the output slew from one cell induces a correlated delay component in subsequent cells." (`timing_pocvm_enable_delay_slew_correlation`) [DOC]; Tempus `delaycal_socv_accuracy_mode low` "Ignores correlation between slews and delays" (default) [DOC]; PrimeTime "Transition variation and constraint variation have to be enabled to get better match with HSPICE monte carlo sims" [fetched blog].
- **Constraint (setup/hold) sigma.** Liberty forbids `sigma_type` on constraint sigma groups, so "constraint = nominal_constraint ± ocv_sigma_rise_constraint" is symmetric; Tempus therefore adds `timing_library_*_sigma_multiplier` / `set_socv_constraint_config` folding factors ("the amount of on-chip-variation pessimism added during cell characterization to the nominal setup/hold tables") [DOC]; PrimeTime `-pocvm_subtract_sigma_factor_from_nominal` exists to remove such folded margin from constraint nominals [DOC]. A documentation-derived note reports an STA-to-SPICE-validated practice of using 3σ for path delay but 3σ–4σ for hold constraints "at low-voltage corners" [secondary, assrs/eda socv-sigma-findings]; a flow template sets `-setup 3 -hold 4.5` [SCRIPT].
- **Graph-based pessimism.** PrimeTime "statistical graph pessimism"/"statistical adjustment" lines and the note that `report_timing -to xyz` bounds but need not match `-from abc -to xyz` [DOC]; Tempus `mean_and_three_sigma_bounded` "ensures that the 3-sigma value of the result always bounds the worst 3-sigma value" [DOC]. "POCV run with GBA provides better correlation with PBA, as it reduces pessimism in GBA. On the other hand, with AOCV, exhaustive PBA had to be run at signoff" [fetched blog].
- **Interconnect (RC) corners.** PrimeTime: "Via variation ... The variation of each via is considered statistically independent and Gaussian ... they introduce both delay and slew variation" (IVM side file per via layer/area) [DOC]; Tempus: RC variation as "global variation as percentage of the nominal delay" (`set_socv_rc_variation_factor`), inter-layer correlation "statistical: all the layers are independent" vs "binomial", same-layer R/C correlation factor, and `-enable_corner_based_beol` "anchor corner-based BEOL" [DOC]. "Conventional interconnect RC extraction methods have over-margins caused by their corner conditions settings ... statistical corner conditions using the independence of variations between process parameters and between interconnect layers are proposed." [SNIPPET, ResearchGate 220243212]. Kahng's group: "Improved signoff methodology with tightened BEOL corners" [title only]. [INFERENCE] Since neither tool ties wire sigma to a global BEOL parameter, TT signoff still needs RC corners (or a tightened/statistical RC corner) for the correlated BEOL component; the statistical RC option covers only the random/independent part.
- **Global mistracking (Vt classes, N/P, FEOL/BEOL).** See Q1: US 11,893,332 mistracking list; Tempus `set_vt_skew_derate`/`timing_enable_vtskew_derate_mode` ("cells of that class operating either faster or slower than the nominal value"); PrimeTime practitioners model "MixedVt / MixedLg" as derates [fetched blog]. [INFERENCE] These are the residual global terms that must be re-added when the SS/FF corner is abandoned; they are not in LVF.
- **IR drop, temperature, aging.** PrimeTime guardbands: "OCV derate ... only accounts for local process variation related derate. Voltage/Temperature and Reliability derates are captured via additional derate specified with -aocvm_guardband/-pocvm_guardband options"; "POCV guardband is applied on both mean delay and sigma delay" [fetched blog + DOC]. Tempus: `set_advanced_variation_mode -scope {vtskew | aging | wire_variation | beol | lle | layout_effect | lde}`, `setDelayCalMode -early_irdrop_data_type {best_average|worst|best|average|worst_average}` (default worst), `power_ir_derated_timing_view`, `timing_derate_voltage_scaling_mode {first_library|snap_to_nearest|interpolate}` [DOC]. Foundry flows keep separate "vt_derates" for IR-drop/temperature on top of LVF [SCRIPT: 32xlr8/gflow comments].
- **Crosstalk.** "POCV analysis is only applied on non-SI cell delay. However, POCV can indirectly change crosstalk delta delay due to the difference in timing window alignment." [fetched blog]; PrimeTime `set_timing_derate -static/-dynamic` cannot be combined with `-pocvm_guardband` [DOC].
- **Characterization/extrapolation.** `timing_pocvm_enable_extrapolation_warning` (UITE-581 when sigma lookup extrapolates > 10 %) [DOC]; LVF validation is a recognized production concern — Semiconductor Engineering, "Timing Library LVF Validation For Production Design Flows" ([semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/timing-library-lvf-validation-for-production-design-flows/)) [title only]; "LVFGen: Efficient Liberty Variation Format (LVF) ..." ([White Rose eprint 226187](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/226187/1/3698364.3705359.pdf)) describes LVF as "four lookup tables (LUTs) ... the nominal values and three statistical moments, mean μ, sigma σ, and skewness γ" with `ocv_mean_shift_cell_rise` ("mean = nominal + mean_shift"), `ocv_std_dev_cell_rise`, `ocv_skewness_cell_rise` [SNIPPET].
- **AOCV→SOCV inference.** Tempus can "infer sensitivity data from AOCV libraries" (`timing_library_infer_socv_from_aocv`, `timing_library_scale_aocv_to_socv_to_n_sigma` default 3) [DOC] — [INFERENCE] a fallback that converts a 3σ depth table back into per-cell sigma and inherits AOCV's chain-based characterization assumptions.

### Inferences
- The "interaction between global corner and local sigma" in the question is real and documented indirectly: LVF sigma tables are corner-specific (characterized per PVT library), constraint sigma is symmetric, and low-V corners are skewed; so a TT-based flow needs (a) sigma from the corner whose tail it is covering, (b) moment-based tables at low V, and (c) explicit separate terms for BEOL, Vt mistracking, IR/temperature/aging that were previously hidden inside the SS corner.
- Temperature inversion (weak-inversion cells becoming slower at low temperature) appears in no statistical-signoff source found; it remains a corner-enumeration problem.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies skewness magnitudes at specific voltages (the Ghanta TAU 2016 deck likely does; it could not be opened).
- No source discusses temperature inversion or aging in combination with statistical/TT signoff.

## Key Question 6: Are there quantitative comparisons of SS+OCV, SS+POCV/LVF, and TT+total-statistical signoff (frequency, area, power, violations, silicon correlation)?

### Takeaway
Only vendor-level aggregate numbers exist in accessible sources (SOCV/LVF "10–15 %" worst-case margin reduction; "150 ps setup / 200 ps hold" average slack improvement of path-based SOCV/LVF over graph-based AOCV; local 3σ impact "~8 % to 3 %" of path delay depending on depth); no accessible publication compares TT+statistical signoff against SS+POCV on frequency/area/power or reports silicon correlation for it.

### Cited Findings
- "SOCV enables SSTA or conventional static timing analysis with on-chip variation tables to provide a more realistic estimation of timing relative to actual silicon performance, which often reduces worst-case timing margins by 10-15%." — [SNIPPET] Cadence Liberate Variety page (above).
- "when using a path-based SOCV/LVF timing signoff methodology rather than a graph-based AOCV timing signoff methodology, you can observe an average improvement of 150ps for setup slack and 200ps for hold slack." — [SNIPPET] Cadence (Tempus datasheet / 16 nm white paper cluster; exact document not confirmed).
- "If you use 3-sigma local variation, its impact on the path delay will be ~8% to 3% depending on the length of the path." (inverter 1σ ≈ 5 %) — [SNIPPET] Semiconductor Engineering, "Process Variation Not A Solved Issue" (above).
- AOCV/POCV depth law `f(n) = k/√n` [gist] — e.g. [INFERENCE] with 3σ/mean = 15 % per stage, a 25-stage path has a 3σ spread of 3 %, matching the semiengineering range.
- PrimeTime report example: two-stage path with per-stage (Mean, Sensit) = (1.00, 3.00) and (1.00, 2.00) yields Path (2.00, 3.61) and corner 12.82 at K = 3, versus 17.00 if the two 3σ increments were simply added (10.00 + 7.00) — a 25 % smaller variation term from quadrature accumulation of just two stages. [DOC + INFERENCE]
- IBM EinsStat: "+40 to 50 percent" CPU versus deterministic timing; "18 seconds ... versus nearly 14 hours" Monte Carlo on a 3,000-gate test chip [SNIPPET, EE Times]. "EinsStat ... allows efficient coverage of the entire process space in a single static timing analysis with reduced timing pessimism" [SNIPPET, EE Times].
- Corner-count baseline: "5×2×4×4 = 160 corners" (Tetelbaum) and "signoff engineers analyzing designs up to 100M instances across as many as 300 mode/corner timing views" at 16/14 nm [SNIPPET, corner-reduction search cluster incl. EE Times "FinFET challenges and solutions" ([eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/finfet-challenges-and-solutions-custom-digital-and-signoff/))].
- Corner-vs-3σ margin illustration (from Q1 inference): 3σ_g + 3σ_l = 6σ vs 3√2σ = 4.24σ when σ_g = σ_l → 41 % over-margin on the variation term. [INFERENCE]

### Inferences
- The only documented sources of margin recovery are (1) local-variation modelling (OCV→AOCV→POCV/LVF, 10–15 %, 150/200 ps) and (2) IBM-style whole-process-space SSTA; the additional gain from moving the base corner from SS to TT would be the global-variation over-margin (the 3σ_g linear term minus its RSS share plus common-mode credit) and is not quantified anywhere accessible.

### Gaps
- No frequency/area/power/violation-count/silicon-correlation comparison of SS+OCV vs SS+POCV/LVF vs TT+statistical was found; the Samsung 10LPP SOCV validation press material gives no numbers; SNUG/CDNLive user papers (the most likely place for such data) were not reachable.
- The origin document for the Cadence "150 ps / 200 ps" claim and its process node were not confirmed.
