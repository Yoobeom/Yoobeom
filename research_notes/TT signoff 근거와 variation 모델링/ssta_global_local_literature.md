## Key Question 1 — The canonical first-order delay model (Visweswariah et al., DAC 2004): how global and local terms are separated, how IBM used it in production, and how statistical results are "projected" to corners

### Takeaway
IBM's canonical form writes every delay/arrival time/slack as a nominal value plus linear sensitivities to a shared set of *global* N(0,1) sources ΔX_i plus one *independently random* N(0,1) term ΔR_a that is unique to each quantity; global terms propagate as correlated sensitivities while independent terms combine by root-sum-square (RSS), and IBM's tools then "project" the resulting canonical slack to a chosen corner (e.g., a 3-sigma worst case) by substituting corner values for the global sources and RSS-ing or summing the random part.

**Equations of the canonical model** (notation of the DAC 2004 paper)
- Canonical form **[VERIFIED in structure; coefficients notation RECONSTRUCTED]**:
  `A = a_0 + Σ_{i=1}^{n} a_i ΔX_i + a_{n+1} ΔR_a`
  where a_0 = mean (nominal) value; ΔX_i ~ N(0,1) are the n *global* sources of variation shared by every timing quantity on the chip (e.g., L_eff, V_t, metal thickness, temperature, Vdd, each normalized to unit variance); a_i = first-order sensitivity to source i; ΔR_a ~ N(0,1) is the *independently random* variable unique to A; a_{n+1} its coefficient. Global vs local separation is therefore purely by *index*: sources 1..n are correlated across all gates (same ΔX_i everywhere), source n+1 is uncorrelated with everything else.
- Variance and covariance **[RECONSTRUCTED]**: `σ_A² = Σ_{i=1}^{n+1} a_i²`, `cov(A,B) = Σ_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i` (independent terms contribute no covariance).
- Sum **[RECONSTRUCTED]**: C = A + B → `c_0 = a_0 + b_0; c_i = a_i + b_i (i=1..n); c_{n+1} = sqrt(a_{n+1}² + b_{n+1}²)`. The last expression is the RSS rule quoted above; along a path of N stages the global part grows linearly in N while the random part grows as sqrt(N).
- Max **[θ and T_A VERIFIED; remaining lines RECONSTRUCTED]**: C = max(A,B):
  `θ = sqrt(σ_A² + σ_B² − 2 cov(A,B)) = sqrt( Σ_{i=1}^{n} (a_i − b_i)² + a_{n+1}² + b_{n+1}² )`
  `T_A = P(A > B) = Φ((a_0 − b_0)/θ)`
  `E[C] = a_0 T_A + b_0 (1 − T_A) + θ φ((a_0 − b_0)/θ)`
  `E[C²] = (a_0² + σ_A²) T_A + (b_0² + σ_B²)(1 − T_A) + (a_0 + b_0) θ φ((a_0 − b_0)/θ)`, `var(C) = E[C²] − E[C]²`
  Re-expression in canonical form: `c_0 = E[C]`, `c_i = T_A a_i + (1 − T_A) b_i` for i = 1..n, and the new independent coefficient is chosen by *variance matching*: `c_{n+1} = sqrt( var(C) − Σ_{i=1}^{n} c_i² )`. The cross-covariance rule `cov(C,W) = T_A cov(A,W) + (1−T_A) cov(B,W)` is Clark's (see Key Question 7; VERIFIED there).
- Slack in canonical form and the "global cancels" effect **[RECONSTRUCTED as an algebraic consequence of the verified form]**: slack `S = RAT − AT` is itself canonical; if launch and capture clock paths share a common segment, that segment's global sensitivities enter AT and RAT with identical sign and cancel in `s_i = rat_i − at_i`, whereas its independent random coefficients do *not* cancel (they RSS), which is why IBM patents speak of a separate "common path credit" and "RSS credit" (see Key Question 5).

### Inferences
- The canonical form is the formal device that lets one analysis carry global and local variation simultaneously: the same ΔX_i multiplies every gate's a_i (perfect correlation → linear accumulation), while each gate's ΔR term is independent (→ RSS accumulation). Projection then evaluates the *same* canonical slack at any corner without re-running timing: `S(corner) = s_0 + Σ_i s_i ΔX_i^{corner} ± k·(random part)`, where the random part is either summed (fully pessimistic, as in the patent example: 5 − 3·2.0 = −1) or RSS-ed (statistical: `s_0 − 3·sqrt(0.9²+0.6²+0.5²) = 5 − 3·1.19 ≈ 1.4`). The two evaluations of the same example illustrate exactly the pessimism that a statistical treatment of the random term removes (this arithmetic is the researcher's, using the patent's numbers).
- "Signing off at TT" in this framework corresponds to setting all global ΔX_i = 0 (nominal) and retaining only the random part with a k-sigma margin (`S_TT,kσ = s_0 − k·a_{n+1}`), or to projecting to a chosen global corner while RSS-ing the local part; the DAC 2004 form makes both a one-line projection of the same object.
- IBM's own trajectory (DAC 2003 critique of pessimistic corner methodology → DAC 2004 canonical form → 2005 EinsStat announcement → 2008 "Back to our roots" methodology that replaces multi-corner + OCV + CPPR with one statistical run → 45 nm ASIC signoff with 62 GB/19 h runs) is the strongest documented industrial case that a single global+local statistical analysis was used as the signoff basis.

## Key Question 2 — Spatial-correlation models: grid + PCA (Chang & Sapatnekar), quad-tree (Agarwal, Blaauw, Zolotov), STAC (Le, Li, Pileggi), robust extraction (Xiong, Zolotov, He), CD variation (Cline et al.), within-die statistical modeling (Hargreaves, Hult, Reda)

### Takeaway
The academic models all decompose a parameter into inter-die + spatially-correlated intra-die + purely random intra-die parts; the grid/PCA and quad-tree models convert the correlated intra-die part into a finite set of independent "global-like" sources (principal components or tree-level variables) that fit the canonical linear form, while the purely random residual is exactly the independent ΔR term. Silicon-extraction papers (Xiong/Zolotov/He, Hargreaves/Hult/Reda) show how the three variance components and a valid correlation function are fitted to measurements.

## Key Question 4 — Corner-based approximations of SSTA: all-corner linear STA (Onaissi & Najm), statistical corners, sensitivity-based corners, statistical library corners, and AOCV/POCV/LVF derates derived from statistics

### Takeaway
Between full SSTA and plain corners sit (i) parameterized STA that propagates affine functions of process parameters over a box and bounds all 2^n corners in one pass (Onaissi–Najm), (ii) "statistical corner" methods that pick the corner in the direction of a design's delay sensitivity, and (iii) the industrial OCV → AOCV → POCV/SOCV → LVF progression, in which global variation stays in the corner library and local random variation is represented by a depth-dependent derate (AOCV, ∝ 1/√n) or an explicit per-cell σ that RSS-accumulates (POCV/LVF).

### Inferences
- The AOCV depth table is a closed-form corner-projection of the canonical model's independent term: for a path of n identical stages with per-stage local σ, the canonical path delay is `n·d_0 + (Σ global terms) + sqrt(n)·σ·ΔR`, so the k-sigma *relative* local margin is `k·σ/(d_0·√n)` — the gist's `f(n) = k/√n`. Global terms are not in the table because they are absorbed by the corner library (ΔX_i fixed at the corner value).
- POCV/LVF is the same decomposition one step closer to SSTA: global held at the corner, local carried as a per-cell σ and RSS-ed; SSTA (IBM) additionally keeps the global terms symbolic so that any corner (including TT) can be projected after the fact and so that global terms cancel in clock-common paths.
- Sensitivity-based statistical corner **[researcher's formalization, standard in the corner-extraction literature; source not retrievable in this session]**: for a linearized metric `f ≈ f_0 + gᵀΔp` with Δp ~ N(0, Σ), the k-sigma worst case lies at `Δp_wc = ±k Σ g / sqrt(gᵀ Σ g)` and gives `f_wc = f_0 ± k sqrt(gᵀ Σ g)`; in the canonical form (Σ = I after normalization) this is exactly `a_0 ± k sqrt(Σ a_i²)`, the RSS projection, whereas a box corner that sets every ΔX_i = ±k gives `a_0 ± k Σ|a_i|` — the linear-sum projection of the IBM patent example. The ratio `Σ|a_i| / sqrt(Σ a_i²)` (between 1 and √(n+1)) quantifies the pessimism of box corners relative to a statistically consistent corner.

## Key Question 5 — The path-depth argument (linear vs √N growth), the averaging/central-limit effect, FMAX mean-shift vs variance, and the clock common-path (CRPR) analogue in SSTA

### Takeaway
Because global sources are perfectly correlated along a path their contribution to path σ grows linearly with stage count N, while independent local terms grow as √N (relative spread ∝ 1/√N); Bowman–Duvall–Meindl (JSSC 2002) showed the system-level consequence — within-die variation mostly shifts the FMAX *mean* (via the max over many critical paths) while die-to-die variation sets the FMAX *variance* — and in canonical-form SSTA the global terms of a shared clock segment cancel automatically in slack while only the independent terms need an explicit common-path credit.

### Cited Findings
- Per-stage independent local variation averages out: "as depth increases (number of gates or transistors in a row), local variation will 'cancel out' and the overall delay will approach a value predicted by the die-to-die variance of the individual cells and effect of the local variance will cancel out such that it has relatively less influence on the variance of the overall delay." — [search summary of Synopsys AOCV white paper](https://www.synopsys.com/content/dam/synopsys/implementation&signoff/white-papers/PrimeTime_AdvancedOCV_WP.pdf); explicit formula `f(n) = (3σ_n)/mean_n = k/√n` — [GitHub gist](https://gist.github.com/brabect1/6281f4cf9fb53002fb17f15fa3bf4f62)
- Corner pessimism statement (secondary): "traditional corner analysis assumes all gates on a path experience worst-case delay simultaneously; in reality, random variations are uncorrelated and average out over long paths." — [ChipFoundryServices KB (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chipfoundryservices/semiconductor-ai-knowledge-base/main/topics/statistical-timing-analysis-ssta.md)
- K. A. Bowman, S. G. Duvall, J. D. Meindl, "Impact of die-to-die and within-die parameter fluctuations on the maximum clock frequency distribution for gigascale integration," IEEE J. - Deterministic CPPR background: "When the data path shares an overlap with the clock path, applying early-late splits for the full path introduces excessive and undesired pessimism, and CPPR attempts to remove this pessimism by tracing these potentially problematic paths and discarding some of the early-late difference along the common sub-path." — [TAU 2014 CPPR contest](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2560519.2565876); "General framework for removal of clock network pessimism" (ICCAD 2002) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/774572.774666); "Clock Path Pessimism: Statistical vs. Logical" — [Design & Reuse](https://www.design-reuse.com/articles/40155/clock-path-pessimism-statistical-vs-logical.html); "Common path pessimism removal: An industry perspective" — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283656049_Common_path_pessimism_removal_An_industry_perspective_Special_Session_Common_Path_Pessimism_Removal)
- Chip-yield arithmetic often used to motivate per-path sigma targets (secondary, illustrative): "Path Yield ... Y_path = Φ(μ_S/σ_S)"; "assuming N independent critical paths, chip yield ≈ Y_path^N; for 1000 critical paths at 3σ each, chip yield = 0.9987^1000 = 27%; requires 4-5σ per-path margin for high chip yield." — [ChipFoundryServices KB (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chipfoundryservices/semiconductor-ai-knowledge-base/main/topics/statistical-timing-analysis-ssta.md)

### Inferences
- The √N law is the whole justification for treating local variation statistically at signoff and for depth-dependent derates; the linear law is the reason global variation cannot be "averaged away" and must be either fixed at a corner or carried as a correlated source.
- In canonical-form SSTA the CRPR effect for *global* variation is free (identical sensitivities subtract in slack), which is why an IBM-style statistical signoff removes the largest part of clock-path OCV pessimism without a path-based CPPR pass; only the independent part of the common segment requires the explicit "common path credit." A TT-corner signoff with local-only statistics therefore behaves like ideal CRPR for the global part by construction.
- Bowman's mean-shift result implies that a "typical" die's FMAX is systematically below the nominal-path prediction by an amount set by WID σ and the number of near-critical paths; a TT-based signoff must budget this mean shift (through the k-sigma local margin on many paths), whereas D2D variation is what corners (SS/FF) or a global-sensitivity projection covers.

## Key Question 6 — Non-Gaussian and nonlinear extensions (quadratic SSTA, non-Gaussian sources, moment matching) and the moment-based LVF motivation at low voltage

### Takeaway
Quadratic and non-Gaussian SSTA (Zhang et al. DAC 2005; Zhan et al. DAC 2005; Chang–Zolotov–Narayan–Visweswariah DAC 2005; Cheng–Xiong–He DAC 2007/TCAD 2009) extend the canonical form because delay is a nonlinear function of sources and becomes skewed even for Gaussian inputs, and the industrial Liberty Variation Format adopted the same idea in moment form (mean shift, σ, skewness) specifically for ultra-low-voltage operation.

### Inferences
- The non-Gaussian literature does not change the global/local split — it changes the *shape* of each term. In the IBM extended form the global terms may be nonlinear/non-Gaussian while the local term is still an independent random variable; in LVF the local term is carried as three moments per cell (mean shift, σ, skew) while global remains in the corner. A TT-corner + LVF flow is therefore "global at nominal, local as skewed distribution."

## Key Question 7 — Reconvergence, correlation and the MAX operation (Clark 1961) and why they matter for global-correlated sources

### Takeaway
Clark's 1961 formulas give the mean, variance and cross-covariance of max(X,Y) for jointly Gaussian X,Y using only their means, variances and correlation; block-based SSTA applies them pairwise, and the correlation term — dominated by shared global sources and by path reconvergence — is what makes the statistical max less pessimistic than a corner max yet still consistent (both operands move together under a global shift).

### Cited Findings
- C. E. Clark, "The Greatest of a Finite Set of Random Variables," Operations Research, vol. 9, no. 2, pp. 145–162, 1961, DOI 10.1287/opre.9.2.145. Verified: addresses "the maximum of variables ξ₁, …, ξₙ that have a joint normal distribution"; "presented formulas and tables for the most general case with n = 2"; "Clark's approach is now fundamental to computing the maximum of Gaussian random variables in statistical timing applications." — [INFORMS PubsOnLine](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.9.2.145); [J-GLOBAL record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201602016797999424)
- Clark's formulas as used in SSTA **[VERIFIED from retrieved text]**: for X₁ ~ N(μ₁,σ₁²), X₂ ~ N(μ₂,σ₂²) with covariance σ₁,₂: `a = sqrt(σ₁² + σ₂² − 2σ₁,₂)`, `α = (μ₁ − μ₂)/a`; first and second moments of max(X₁,X₂): `ν₁ = μ₁ Q(−α) + μ₂ Q(α) + a ψ(α)`, `ν₂ = (μ₁² + σ₁²) Q(−α) + (μ₂² + σ₂²) Q(α) + (μ₁ + μ₂) a ψ(α)`, where ψ is the standard normal PDF and Q the Gaussian tail function (Q(−α) = Φ(α)); the max "is then approximated as a Gaussian random variable with mean ν₁ and variance ν₂ − ν₁²." Cross-covariance with any jointly Gaussian W: `cov(W, Z) = Q·cov(W, X) + (1 − Q)·cov(W, Y)`, "where Q is the 'tightness' parameter" (= Φ(α), the probability that X is the larger). — [search summary citing arXiv:2401.03588 and Iowa State c27.pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.03588); [Iowa State "Timing Yield Estimation Using SSTA" (blocked)](https://home.engineering.iastate.edu/~cnchu/pubs/c27.pdf); [Gwern, order statistics](https://gwern.net/order-statistic)
- D. Sinha, H. Zhou, N. V. Shenoy, "Advances in Computation of the Maximum of a Set of Gaussian Random Variables," IEEE TCAD vol. 26, no. 8, pp. 1522–1533, Aug. 2007 (conference version ISQED 2006). Verified abstract: "quantifies the approximation error when results obtained by Clark (1961) are employed to compute the maximum (max) of Gaussian random variables"; "A finite lookup table can be used to store these errors. Based on the error computations, approaches to different orderings for pairwise max operations on a set of Gaussians are proposed"; also "an approach to compute the tightness probabilities of Gaussian random variables with dynamic runtime-accuracy tradeoff options." — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TCAD.2007.893544); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Advances-in-computation-of-the-maximum-of-a-set-of-Sinha-Zhou/57447ce26ec81e33adb41a02cdec943f4950454a); [ISQED 2006 PDF (blocked)](https://users.ece.northwestern.edu/~haizhou/publications/isqed06sinha.pdf); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1613154)
- Reconvergence/path-sharing correlation is captured by the canonical form itself: the DAC 2004 model "accommodates ... correlation due to path sharing or reconvergent fan-out" (Key Question 1), and STAC "can account for correlations from process parameters and re-converging paths" (Key Question 2). — [DAC 2004 summary](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~alanmi/research/timing/papers/sta_ibm.pdf); [STAC](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/996566.996665)
- Moment matching variants: "In block-based SSTA, the moment matching is performed to compute the canonical form representing max(X,Y). For example, researchers match the mean, variance and covariance, while others match the raw moments." — [search summary (Chen thesis, Northwestern)](https://users.ece.northwestern.edu/~haizhou/publications/chen-thesis.pdf)

### Inferences
- In canonical form the covariance entering Clark's `a` (= θ of Key Question 1) is `Σ_i a_i b_i`, i.e., entirely due to shared global/regional sources; two paths that depend on the same global sources have small θ and a max close to the larger mean (little "max-induced" mean shift), whereas paths dominated by independent local terms have large θ and a larger positive mean shift `a·ψ(α)`. This is the statistical version of the observation that global variation is common-mode across competing paths while local variation creates real races.
- The cross-covariance rule is what allows a global source's sensitivity to survive the max (`c_i = T_A a_i + (1−T_A) b_i`), so a global shift still moves the result coherently — which is precisely why the final canonical slack can be projected to a global corner after the max operations.

### Gaps
- The original 1961 paper text and Clark's tables were not accessible; the formulas are quoted from SSTA re-statements.

## Key Question 8 — Explicit comparisons of "worst-case corner + OCV" vs "statistical global + local," and discussion of typical/nominal (TT) signoff with statistical margins

### Takeaway
Direct comparisons found are mostly industrial (IBM's DAC 2003 and ASP-DAC 2008 papers; vendor white papers; trade-press articles) and consistently describe corner+OCV as pessimistic because it applies a uniform derate that ignores the √N averaging of local variation and the common-mode cancellation of global variation; no academic paper was found that argues *specifically* for TT-corner signoff, but the IBM projection framework and the POCV/LVF flow both instantiate "nominal (or corner) global + statistical local" as the signoff object.

### Cited Findings
- C. Visweswariah, "Death, taxes and failing chips," DAC 2003: present-day methodologies cope with variability "in a manner that is onerous, pessimistic and risky." — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/775832.775921)
- R. Chen et al., "Static timing: Back to our roots," ASP-DAC 2008: multi-corner timing, OCV derating coefficients and path-based CPPR "destroy the benefits of linear run-time and incrementality possessed by classical static timing"; the proposed statistical methodology offers "comprehensive modeling of process variations" while "retaining those key benefits" and "is compatible with existing characterization methods and scales well to large chip designs." — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/4327328_Static_timing_Back_to_our_roots)
- Corner-based methods "can be simple but often involve severe pessimism, and ... can be both pessimistic and optimistic compared to actual circuit behavior in applications involving races between multiple paths"; "On-chip variation can affect different paths differently, rendering worst-case corner based margin estimates severely inadequate"; "OCV adds pessimism to the design through analysis to cover variation that could happen between clock routes of devices spread around the chip." — [search summary aggregating patents US 10,235,484 / US 8,555,222 and trade articles](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8555222)
- Trade-press coverage of the corner-vs-statistical debate (titles verified; text not readable): "Industry Ready To Sign On To Statistical Timing Signoff" — [Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/news/products/article/21756569/industry-ready-to-sign-on-to-statistical-timing-signoff); "Timing Analysis Rounds The Corner To Statistics" — [Electronic Design](https://www.electronicdesign.com/news/products/article/21775678/timing-analysis-rounds-the-corner-to-statistics); "Less Pessimism, Please" (Extreme DA, Aug. 2011) — [EE Journal](https://www.eejournal.com/article/20110801-extremeda/); "The Latest in Static Timing Analysis with Variation Modeling" — [SemiWiki](https://semiwiki.com/eda/cadence/5641-the-latest-in-static-timing-analysis-with-variation-modeling/).
- "Statistical timing analysis ... allowing accurate estimation of the probability distribution of circuit performance ... in a single timing analysis run" vs conventional analysis that "may generate over-optimistic results on criticality-dependent paths." — [Blaauw et al. TCAD 2008 summary](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/TCAD.2007.907047)
- The statistical library-corner patent describes the signoff comparison object explicitly: "comparing single statistical corner delays with chip sign-off statistical delay requirements." — [US 8,413,095](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8413095)
- IBM 45 nm ASIC SSTA signoff: "SSTA signoff timing methodology for 45nm ASICs using EinsTimer EinsStat"; "new methods have been developed to maximize product yield and improve timing accuracy"; "chip timing analysis adds margin for variation; however, this leads to a loss in possible processing performance." — [academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/4136360/Timing_Closure_in_45Nanometer_ASICs_Using_Statistical_Static_Timing_Analysis_Design_Methodology)
- Secondary numeric claims on pessimism (unsourced marketing-grade, include only with caveat): corner analysis "over-estimates path delay by 15-30% at 7nm/5nm"; AOCV "10-20% less pessimistic than flat OCV"; POCV "20-30% less pessimistic than AOCV"; "POCV is increasingly required for timing signoff at 7nm/5nm; foundries provide POCV libraries"; "most designs still use deterministic STA with AOCV/POCV as a compromise; full SSTA used primarily for critical blocks or advanced nodes." — [ChipFoundryServices KB (GitHub)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chipfoundryservices/semiconductor-ai-knowledge-base/main/topics/statistical-timing-analysis-ssta.md)

### Inferences
- Technical basis for a TT (nominal-global) signoff that still reflects global and local variation at once, as supported by the sources above: (1) write the timing quantity in canonical form (DAC 2004); (2) the global sources are perfectly correlated across the chip, so (a) they are covered by evaluating the *same* canonical slack at the global corner values of interest (IBM projection) — evaluating at ΔX_i = 0 is the TT projection, and (b) for the launch/capture-clock common segment they cancel identically in slack (statistical CRPR); (3) the local term is independent per stage, accumulates as √N, and is covered by a k-sigma RSS margin (`s_0 − k·sqrt(Σ random coefficients²)`) rather than a linear-sum derate; (4) commercial POCV/LVF signoff implements exactly (2a)+(3) with global fixed by the library corner and local given by per-cell σ (or three moments), so "TT + POCV/LVF" is the industrial instantiation of "nominal global + statistical local"; (5) whether the global part must additionally be projected to SS/FF (or to a sensitivity-based statistical corner) is a yield-target decision, since D2D variation sets the FMAX *variance* across dies (Bowman) and cannot be averaged out along paths.
- The pessimism ordering implied by all sources is flat OCV (linear-sum derate, no depth or commonality awareness) > AOCV (depth/distance tables) > POCV/SOCV/LVF (per-cell σ, RSS) ≳ full SSTA (adds symbolic global terms, criticality, projection), with SSTA additionally offering yield/criticality diagnostics rather than a single slack.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that (i) reports a side-by-side pessimism number for "SS corner + OCV" vs "canonical global+local SSTA" on the same design with a stated methodology, or (ii) explicitly defends TT-corner signoff with statistical margins; the closest are the IBM methodology papers (which replace corners rather than justify TT) and vendor/trade material. The percentages quoted from the GitHub knowledge base are unattributed and should not be reported as findings.
- The Electronic Design, EE Journal, Design & Reuse and Cadence/Synopsys pages could not be opened, so quotes from vendors or from Visweswariah in those articles are unavailable.
