## Does Samsung Electronics (or Samsung Foundry) / SK hynix hold patents on TT signoff, statistical corners, "global corner + local variation" margins, or LVF/OCV derate generation?

### Takeaway
One highly relevant Samsung patent surfaced — US 9,977,845 "Method of performing static timing analysis for an integrated circuit", whose claims (per search-engine extraction of the official USPTO document) combine a library holding local random variation and global variation information with a statistical sum of local and global variation values in slack computation, with global variation values scaled to 1σ and operating-speed distributions at 3σ — but the page could not be opened, so it is search-snippet verified only. No SK hynix patent in this space was found, and no pre-2014 Samsung timing patent appeared in three levels of citation crawling.

## Does TSMC hold patents on statistical corner / total-variation corner generation, LVF-based signoff, global-and-local variation in timing libraries, or statistical corner selection?

### Takeaway
Yes — the strongest verified item in the whole list is TSMC's US 8,275,584 (priority Dec 2006), which claims generating σ_total from intra-die and inter-die test-pattern data, splitting it into σ_global and σ_local (one measured directly, the other obtained by removing the first from σ_total), and generating separate corner models for global and for local variation; TSMC also holds stage-based / stage-weight OCV derate patents (US 8,365,115; US 2011/0035715) whose derate tables are built from Monte Carlo 3σ/mean ratios and, in one figure, explicitly characterized "SSG [w/o global variation]" at "TT" transition — i.e., local OCV derates derived with global variation removed.

### Cited Findings
- **US 8,275,584 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Unified model for process variations in integrated circuits" — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, Ltd. — inventors (OCR of front page): Chung-Kai Lin (Taipei), Cheng-… Hsiao (Hsinchu), Sally Liu (Hsinchu) — filed Dec 12, 2006; published as US 2008/0140363 A1 (Jun 12, 2008); granted Sep 25, 2012 — 25 claims, independent claims 1, 14, 21 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8275584.pdf); [A1 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US20080140363.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8275584B2/en).
  - Summary: test patterns (MOS devices, resistors, capacitors, memory cells, wires) are placed on many identical dies; intra-die and inter-die data are collected; σ_total is generated from both; one of σ_global/σ_local is "appointed" as the first sigma and computed directly (σ_local from intra-die data or σ_global from inter-die data, as "a single numerical value"), and the second sigma is obtained "by removing the first sigma from the sigma_total" (flow charts show σ_local = sqrt(σ_total² − σ_global²) and vice versa); then "a corner model for global variations based on sigma_global and the set of inter-die data" and "a corner model for local variations based on sigma_local and the set of intra-die data" are generated, with Monte Carlo generation of global, local and mismatch variation. The background states that conventional modeling "does not separate global and local variations", that applying local variations on top of total-variation corners causes "double counting of local variations", that existing corner models assume "all devices are sampled at a same process corner", and that σ_local from the 1/sqrt(W·L) mismatch rule can exceed σ_total.
  - Claim 1 gist: creating a set of test patterns on a die; collecting intra-die data and inter-die data; generating σ_total; appointing one of σ_global and σ_local as a first sigma (a single value) generated from the corresponding data set; generating the second sigma by removing the first from σ_total; generating a corner model for global variations from σ_global and the inter-die data; generating a corner model for local variations from σ_local and the intra-die data. Claim 14: per-die median values form the inter-die set → σ_global; σ_local = σ_total minus σ_global. Claim 21: per-die intra-die sigmas are averaged → σ_local; σ_global obtained by removing σ_local from σ_total.
- **US 2011/0035715 A1 [PDF-verified]** — "System and method for on-chip-variation analysis" — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Ltd. — Lee-Chung Lu, Chung-Hsing Wang, Yuan-Te Hou — filed Aug 10, 2009; published Feb 10, 2011 — independent claims 1, 10, 16 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US20110035715.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20110035715A1/en).
  - Summary: each cell's rise and fall arcs get a "state dependent stage weight" (delay normalized to a reference cell); the weights along a path are summed and used as the index into a single "unified stage-derating factor table" of OCV derating factors; the table is generated once from a reference multi-stage circuit by Monte Carlo simulation, "the ratio of the 3-sigma delay to the mean is used as the derating factor" at each stage count N; the total path delay is derated accordingly. Background cites the stage-based OCV method (US 2010/0229137) as still pessimistic.
  - Claim 1 gist: apparatus storing state-dependent stage weights per rising/falling arc per library cell; an adder summing the weights of the cells in a path; a table of OCV derating factors indexed by the sum; means for computing the total path delay with the corresponding derating factor.
- **US 8,365,115 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "System and method for performance modeling of integrated circuits" — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., Ltd. — Louis Chao-Chiuan Liu (Hsinchu), Morly Hsieh (Sunnyvale), Dei-Pei Liu (Taipei) — provisional 61/157,951 filed Mar 6, 2009; filed Dec 3, 2009; published US 2010/0229137 A1 (Sep 9, 2010); granted Jan 29, 2013 — independent claims 1, 6, 18 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8365115.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8365115B2/en).
  - Summary: stage-based OCV (SBOCV): the number of non-common timing-path elements (buffer stages) is counted and a timing de-rate factor is assigned "solely based upon" that number from a look-up table, reducing pessimism for long paths and increasing robustness for short ones. FIG. 6a prints the table-generation setup: "OCV MARGIN (PS) 50 / OCV % 5.4% / N*SIGMA 5.0 / SBOCV MARGIN (PS) 50.0 / TECHNOLOGY 45LP / VARIATION TYPE SSG [W/O GLOBAL VARIATION] / CELL TYPE CKBD48WP9T / TRANSITION @TT 100P", i.e., the derate table is characterized at the SS-global corner with global variation excluded and slew set at TT; FIG. 6b/7 plot skew vs. stage count.
  - Claim 1 gist: computing the number of non-common timing-path elements; assigning a de-rate factor solely based on that number with a stage de-rate factor select unit; computing and storing the timing analysis. Claim 6 is a sign-off style claim: "a method for approving an integrated circuit for fabrication" in which every path is de-rated by a stage-based OCV engine and the design is accepted when performance data meet requirements.
- **US 7,802,209 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Method for reducing timing libraries for intra-die model in statistical static timing analysis" — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. — Louis Chaochiuan Liu, Hsing-Chien Huang, Ssu-Chia Chang — filed Jul 13, 2007; published US 2009/0019409 A1 (Jan 15, 2009); granted Sep 21, 2010 — independent claims 1, 7, 13 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US7802209.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7802209B2/en).
  - Summary: background notes corner analysis "has been successfully used to model die-to-die variations" but cannot model within-die variation, and that SSTA with an intra-die model needs many libraries; the method identifies devices turned on during a predetermined (worst) operation and uses only those devices' libraries for delay calculation, reducing library count. Claim 1 gist: identify turned-on devices; choose only their libraries (files with timing/logical information for cell collections); calculate at least a time delay using only those libraries; incorporate the calculation into synthesis or layout.
- **US 8,707,230 B1 [PDF-verified]** — "Method and system for semiconductor simulation" — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company — Wei-Yi Hu, Chin-Cheng Kuo, Cheng-Hung Yeh, Jui-Feng Kuan, Yi-Kan Cheng — filed Mar 11, 2013; granted Apr 22, 2014 — independent claims 1, 15, 17 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8707230.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8707230B1/en). Summary: Monte Carlo simulation combining a device process model (each device variable defines a probability distribution of active-device-level variation) with a conductive-line model and/or a multi-patterning model (mask-misalignment-induced coupling variation) to identify parasitic couplings; statistical process modeling rather than timing-corner derivation (low relevance to signoff margins).
- Not relevant but TSMC-assigned and checked: US 8,589,831 B1 "Skew sensitive calculation for misalignment from multi patterning" (Chih-Hsien Chang et al., filed Jul 30, 2012, granted Nov 19, 2013) — lithography skew, not timing variation — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8589831.pdf).
- Search summaries also attributed "Statistical corner evaluation for complex on-chip variation model" (US 8,555,222 / US 8,843,864, SCS-OCV, "pessimism at nominal and targeted N-sigma corner") to TSMC; the official PDF of US 8,555,222 shows assignee Synopsys, Inc. and applicants Jiayong Le, Mustafa Celik, Guy Maor et al. — [US8555222 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8555222.pdf).

### Inferences
- US 8,275,584 is the clearest chipmaker claim to (b) and (c): it claims the global/local variance decomposition σ_local² = σ_total² − σ_global² and the generation of *separate* global and local corner models, and its background explicitly frames the double-counting problem that motivates "global corner + local statistical margin" signoff.
- TSMC's SBOCV family (US 8,365,115 → US 2011/0035715) documents the practice of characterizing local-variation derates with global variation removed ("SSG w/o global variation") and mapping N·σ (5.0σ in the example) OCV margins to per-stage derates — direct evidence for (d).

### Gaps
- Grant status and any continuation of US 2011/0035715 could not be checked; TSMC's LVF-era filings (2015+) were unreachable.

## Intel (and Altera, acquired by Intel in 2015): sigma-based/statistical timing signoff, corner reduction, global/local decomposition?

### Takeaway
No Intel Corporation timing-variation patent was reachable or surfaced in crawling (Intel hits were leakage/noise patents); two Altera patents (now Intel-owned) — multi-corner optimization accounting for intra-corner variability (US 7,290,232) and common-clock-path-pessimism analysis (US 7,926,019) — are verified but only moderately relevant.

### Cited Findings
- **US 7,290,232 B1 [PDF-verified]** — "Optimizing long-path and short-path timing and accounting for manufacturing and operating condition variability" — Altera Corporation — Ryan Fung, Vaughn Betz — application 11/002,976 (filed 2004); granted Oct 30, 2007 — independent claims 1, 16 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US7290232.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7290232B1/en). Summary: each process/operating corner is a set of timing delays and "performance capabilities can vary within each logic device… these variations are sometimes referred to as intra-corner variations", so each corner holds a range of delays; timing values and slacks are transformed between corners and amalgamated so a single-corner compiler can optimize for multiple corners while meeting long- and short-path constraints. Claim 1 gist: identify potentially critical paths from delays and max/min constraints that "include timing constraints for a plurality of corners", and revise the design accordingly.
- **US 7,926,019 B1 [PDF-verified]** — "Common clock path pessimism analysis for circuit designs using clock tree networks" — Altera Corporation — Ajay K. Ravi — filed Feb 29, 2008; granted Apr 12, 2011 — independent claims 1, 13, 14, 18 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US7926019.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7926019B1/en). Claim 1 gist: transform a clock network into a clock tree; populate it with source/destination registers; enumerate Common Clock Path Pessimism (CCPP) groups sharing a nearest common ancestor; eliminate CCPP per group; output results (relevant to removing common-path derate pessimism, i.e., the global/common-mode part of OCV on shared clock segments).
- Intel Corporation documents that surfaced in citation crawling were unrelated to timing variation: US 6,169,419 "Method and apparatus for reducing standby leakage current using a transistor stack effect", US 6,300,819, US 6,721,924 "Noise and power optimization in high performance circuits", US 5,936,867, US 6,128,769 — [US6169419 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US6169419.pdf); [US6721924 PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US6721924.pdf).

### Inferences

## Qualcomm, Apple, ARM, AMD, NVIDIA, Broadcom (incl. LSI/Agere), MediaTek, Marvell, Xilinx, TI, NXP/Freescale/Philips: statistical OCV, variation-aware signoff, corner selection, common-mode pessimism?

### Takeaway
Verified hits cluster in the Broadcom lineage (Agere → LSI → Avago/Broadcom): location-based OCV factors (US 7,399,648), corner-derate STA with clock conservatism reduction (US 7,480,881), internal-depth-aware derating (US 2013/0152034), K-factor derating between characterized base corners and off-corners (US 2009/0217226), multi-corner variability/margin risk analysis (US 7,181,713), and statistical device models derived from worst-case corner files (US 6,356,861); plus Freescale/NXP (AOCV derates from characterization sensitivity data, US 8,656,331), Texas Instruments (gradient AOCV depth tables, US 8,806,413) and Philips/NXP (derating-factor determination, US 7,010,475). Only a memory-yield patent was found for Qualcomm; nothing reachable for Apple, ARM, AMD, NVIDIA, MediaTek, Marvell or Xilinx.

### Cited Findings
- **US 8,656,331 B1 [PDF-verified]** — "Timing margins for on-chip variations from sensitivity data" — Freescale Semiconductor, Inc. (now NXP) — Savithri Sundareswaran, Surya Veeraraghavan — filed Feb 14, 2013; granted Feb 18, 2014 — independent claims 1, 9, 16 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8656331.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8656331B1/en). Claim 1 gist: execute transistor-level simulation of a library cell with process parameters; collect sensitivity data quantifying performance-metric changes when parameters are adjusted; generate derate factors from the sensitivity data; test the IC design by STA using those derate factors.
- **US 8,806,413 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Gradient AOCV methodology enabling graph-based timing closure with AOCV timing models" — Texas Instruments Incorporated — Raashid Moin Shaikh, Vishnuraj Arukat Rajan — provisional 61/702,044 (Sep 17, 2012); filed Sep 17, 2013; published US 2014/0082576 A1 (Mar 20, 2014); granted Aug 12, 2014 — independent claims 1, 7 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8806413.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8806413B2/en). Claim 1 gist: place and route a cell set, check timing closure using cell delays derated by a depth-indexed table whose derates for depths below k are identical, iterate, and construct the circuit.
- **US 7,399,648 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Methods and apparatus for determining location-based on-chip variation factor" — Agere Systems Inc. (later LSI → Avago/Broadcom) — Denis Bzowy (Anzing, DE) — filed May 6, 2005; published US 2006/0253820 A1 (Nov 9, 2006); granted Jul 15, 2008 — independent claims 1, 15, 20 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US7399648.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7399648B2/en). Claim 1 gist exactly that combination.
- **US 7,480,881 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Method and computer program for static timing analysis with delay de-rating and clock conservatism reduction" — LSI Logic Corporation — Alexander Tetelbaum, Ruben Molina, Subodh Bhike — filed Aug 18, 2006; published US 2008/0046848 A1 (Feb 21, 2008); granted Jan 20, 2009 — independent claims 1, 18 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US7480881.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7480881B2/en). Claim 1 gist: receive min and max stage delays for two corners; compute setup slack as a function of T_clk, launch/capture/data path delays and a first de-rating factor Y1; compute hold slack with a second de-rating factor Y2; output both slacks.
- **US 2013/0152034 A1 [PDF-verified]** — "System and method for reducing integrated circuit timing derating" — LSI Corporation (now Broadcom) — Alexander Tetelbaum — filed Dec 9, 2011; published Jun 13, 2013 — independent claims 1, 8, 14, 18 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US20130152034.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130152034A1/en). ## Japanese companies (Renesas/NEC Electronics, NEC, Toshiba, Fujitsu, Matsushita/Panasonic, Hitachi, Sony, Mitsubishi): SSTA, global/local (chip-to-chip / within-chip) variation, statistical libraries, statistical corners

### Takeaway
This is the richest verified group: NEC Electronics' statistical cell library with separately defined global (chip-to-chip) and local (within-chip) permissible variation ranges (US 2009/0019408, JP 2007-182664), NEC's systematic+random OCV delay calculation (US 7,526,399) and sigma-per-arc library (US 6,526,541), Renesas' unified-path σ_chip = f(σ_random, σ_systematic) OCV derivation (US 7,512,920), Toshiba's SSTA-derived per-path corner conditions (US 2009/0249272) and statistical cell-library generator (US 8,196,077), Fujitsu's stage-count-dependent in-chip variation coefficient at 3σ (US 2004/0254776) and OCV coefficient from wiring variation (US 2008/0178134), and Matsushita/Panasonic's statistical delay library (US 7,239,997) and yield-based derating-factor ("design margin") setting (US 2004/0261044). No Sony or Hitachi variation-timing patent was found.

### Inferences
- NEC Electronics US 2009/0019408 is the closest Japanese analogue to "library characterization with separated global and local variation" (item e): its permissible ranges are ±3σ along principal components and are split into global (between chips) and local (within chip) ranges that the core-macro model must cover jointly.

## Cross-cutting: IDM/EDA context patents that the report writer may need (IBM, Magma, Synopsys, Cadence) and the unverified candidate list

### Takeaway
IBM (an IDM until 2015, not on the requested list) holds the most explicit verified "chip-mean + OCV" statistical-corner signoff claims (US 8,413,095: a single worst-case Liberty library whose statistical corners are derived from chip-mean process, OCV, aging and N/P-mistrack parameters and compared with a chip sign-off statistical delay requirement; US 8,141,012: project a full-parameter-space SSTA run onto selected corners for closure; US 7,555,740: sensitivity credit from best/worst corner slacks and sigma points). The SCS-OCV "N-sigma corner" family, DS-OCV and "statistical OCV" patents that search engines attributed to chipmakers are Synopsys/Cadence/Magma EDA patents. Several post-2014 candidates remain unverified.

### Cited Findings
- **US 8,413,095 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Statistical single library including on chip variation for rapid timing and power analysis" — International Business Machines Corporation — John P. Dubuque, Eric A. Foreman, Peter A. Habitz, Jeffrey G. Hemmett, Amol A. Joshi, Christopher J. Kiegle, William J. Wright, Vladimir Zolotov — filed Feb 21, 2012; granted Apr 2, 2013 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8413095.pdf); [GP](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8413095B2/en). Claim 1 gist: receive libraries for all cells with ranges of supply voltage, load, slew, temperature "and any of chip mean and OCV process, aging, and N/P mistrack parameters"; apply a statistical model and a statistical timing tool across those ranges "to determine WC statistical corners for delay and power"; enter the per-corner delay/power into a statistical single worst-case (Liberty) library; abstract adds that each corner delay is "compared with a chip sign-off statistical delay requirement of a test macro".
- **US 8,141,012 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Timing closure on multiple selective corners in a single statistical timing run" — IBM — Nathan C. Buck et al. — filed Aug 27, 2009; published US 2011/0055793 A1; granted Mar 20, 2012 — [PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/pdfs/US8141012.pdf). Claim 1 gist: run one SSTA over the full parameter space P1..Pn, select k corners per path, project the statistical results to deterministic values at those corners (plus the remaining sub-space), and close timing on the worst-slack corners.
- **US 7,555,740 B2 [PDF-verified]** — "Method and system for evaluating statistical sensitivity credit in path-based hybrid multi-corner static timing analysis" — IBM — Nathan C.
